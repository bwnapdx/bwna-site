## Community News

### Beaumont Crossing: One Last Weekend, Then a Review

The plaza's trial run ends September 30, and its last event is the **Summer Send-Off on Saturday, September 26**. Cider, pastries, and hands-on fun for kids from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m., the [Oregon Symphonic Band](/events/2026-09-26-oregon-symphonic-band-plaza) from 2 to 3:30 p.m., and [Patina](/events/2026-09-26-patina-plaza) closing out the season from 5 to 7 p.m. Bring chairs and blankets!

After next week the barriers and furniture will come off the street at NE 44th and Fremont.

The Portland Bureau of Transportation (PBOT) is conducting a survey to evaluate the pilot project and will publish its findings. Plaza partners BWNA, the Beaumont Business Association, and PBOT will then determine if Beaumont Crossing will return as a permanent member of the city's Public Street Plaza Program next summer.

PBOT's Beaumont Crossing survey is still open, and we encourage you to weigh in about your plaza experience. Tell PBOT what you think of the plaza: [Pilot Plaza Survey: Beaumont Crossing](https://www.surveymonkey.com/r/beaumontcrossing).

PBOT's assessment, when publicized, will be summarized on our [Public Plaza](/public-plaza) page. Questions and comments are always welcome at [plaza@bwnapdx.org](mailto:plaza@bwnapdx.org).

### Emergency Preparedness: Where to Start

Bec Lawson, who leads the Beaumont-Wilshire/Alameda Neighborhood Emergency Team (NET), came to BWNA's Sept. 14 board meeting to explain how NET works. NET members are neighbors trained by the Portland Bureau of Emergency Management and Portland Fire & Rescue to help in the window immediately after a disaster, before professional responders can reach everyone.

NET is planning a series of community preparedness meetings for Beaumont-Wilshire and Alameda, and the first could come as early as January.

Bec also made the case that trained responders are only part of the picture. NET wants to hear from neighbors who can bring useful professional skills to the effort, doctors and electricians among them, whether or not they take the training.

To reach our NET team, write to [bec@friendsofportlandnet.org](mailto:bec@friendsofportlandnet.org). To hear about team meetings, [sign up with the team](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSe946_kx5B0uW5Qzk9nto7qmbakLHt_fomHSzBSG9JKMvWi3Q/viewform), which adds you to the group where meetings are announced.

Even if you don't attend a training or learning session, the single most useful thing you can do is store water. The general rule is **a minimum of one gallon per person per day for 14 days**, or 14 gallons per person. Store extra for pets.

While you are at it, keep unscented bleach and a dropper on hand for sanitizing questionable water. Find all you need to know about storage and treatment of emergency water at [regionalh2o.org](https://regionalh2o.org). There's also a full emergency supply list at [publicalerts.org](https://www.publicalerts.org/supplies).

### Have (Too Much) Fruit Every Year?

Plums or peaches too high in the tree to reach, so they go unpicked? Apples rotting on the ground after you've had your fill? Too many grapes or berries? Fruit trees need pruning and you've no idea how to start? The nonprofit Portland Fruit Tree Project can help solve all of these problems for you.

The organization picks fruit for you, donates what you do not want to area food banks, and sends people out to prune your trees or to teach you how, which makes future picking easier and the trees more productive.

At BWNA's September 14 board meeting, Portland Fruit Tree Project board member Michelle Haberlach shared that, as of that date, 700 volunteers had harvested 10,000 pounds of fruit around the city.

Pruning of fruit trees is best done in the winter months, so now is the time to get on the list. Register your trees for harvest at [portlandfruit.org/request-a-harvest](https://portlandfruit.org/request-a-harvest/) or email [treecare@portlandfruit.org](mailto:treecare@portlandfruit.org).

### Inner Eastside Zoning: Three Ways to Weigh In This Month

The City of Portland's Bureau of Planning and Sustainability (BPS) has launched neighborhood outreach efforts to get input from residents and businesses on the Inner Eastside Zoning Project. The Portland City Council is requesting a report on the topic by summer 2028, a year later than originally requested to allow more time for public input.

The intent of the Inner Eastside Project is to bring more housing types into close-in eastside neighborhoods. The study area includes a significant slice of Beaumont-Wilshire, just south of Fremont and east of NE 33rd Avenue. Nothing has been decided.

Opportunities for public input this month include:

- **Friday, October 2, 2–6 p.m., [Planning in the Park](/events/2026-10-02-inner-eastside-planning-in-the-park) at Grant Park.** A casual drop-in session at the Grant Park picnic shelter where you can learn about the Inner Eastside Zoning Project and share your thoughts and priorities to help shape the future of the neighborhood. Come for five minutes or stay an hour. Rain or shine. BPS staff want to hear what matters most to you.
- **Wednesday, October 7, 6 p.m., [online information session](/events/2026-10-07-inner-eastside-online-session).** Same opportunity as above, but join in from your living room. [Registration required](https://us06web.zoom.us/meeting/register/RcJtME-wTiWjQ2XM5mQYmw).
- **A questionnaire**, which BPS says will be released this month. We will link it as soon as it is live.

Learn more and sign up for project updates on the [City's project page](https://www.portland.gov/bps/planning/inner-eastside-zoning-project/about).
