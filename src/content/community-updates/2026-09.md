## Community News

### Beaumont Crossing: Tell PBOT What You Think

The trial run of Beaumont Crossing is still just that, a trial, and no decision has been made about the plaza's future. The best way to help determine its future is to respond to PBOT's survey, which is intended to gauge the effects of the plaza on neighboring residents and businesses. We hope you'll take a few minutes and complete the [2026 Plaza Survey](https://www.surveymonkey.com/r/plazas). It's also available in [Spanish](https://www.surveymonkey.com/r/plazas?lang=es).

Want to host a community event at Beaumont Crossing? The city has a straightforward [guide](https://www.portland.gov/transportation/planning/plazas/community-events-plaza), and the plaza calendar has openings. [Event applications](https://www.portland.gov/transportation/permitting/portland-streets/apply-community-event-permit) must be submitted at least 7 days before your event.

You can also read up on the latest about the plaza, including PBOT's assessment results when they're available, by visiting our [Public Plaza](/public-plaza) page. And, of course, your questions or comments are always welcome at [plaza@bwnapdx.org](mailto:plaza@bwnapdx.org).

### Meet Graeme Rudd, Our New Transportation Committee Chair

Graeme Rudd has stepped up to lead BWNA's Transportation Committee, and he has hit the ground running by researching, fact finding, and getting on calls with contacts at PBOT.

One thing he has already turned up may surprise you. NE Fremont Street is designated part of the city's **High Crash Network**, a set of 30 streets and intersections identified as the city's most dangerous for traffic fatalities and serious injuries. Graeme wants to understand why Fremont has earned that designation and what PBOT can do to help keep the street safe, and he's convinced the more data we bring, the more effectively we can advocate for the neighborhood.

That is where you come in. Graeme wants to hear what neighbors are actually concerned about when it comes to traffic and transit here in Beaumont-Wilshire. Send him a note at [pdx.stmptwn@gmail.com](mailto:pdx.stmptwn@gmail.com).

### Emergency Preparedness: Two Ways to Join In

Our September 14 board meeting (details above) will feature Bec Lawson, who leads the Beaumont-Wilshire/Alameda Neighborhood Emergency Team. NET members are residents trained by the Portland Bureau of Emergency Management and Portland Fire & Rescue to help their neighbors after a disaster, in the window before professional responders can reach everyone.

Becoming an active NET volunteer involves completing a city-sponsored basic training program of roughly 28 hours and volunteering at least 12 hours of time yearly. Training is offered only a few times a year. This year's session is full, and the next round is not expected until early 2027.

There is a second way to get involved. **Affiliated Team Volunteers** support NETs without holding full "active" NET status or having to complete the full basic training. No special skills are required. It is an easier ramp into the NET orbit, and a good way to meet the neighbors you would be working alongside if an emergency were to happen.

Come meet Bec on September 14 and ask how our team puts Affiliated Team Volunteers to work, or read the details on the [Portland NET wiki](https://volunteerpdx.net/index.php/Affiliated_Team_Volunteers_%28ATVs%29). And if you do want the full training, you can [start the application and preparedness videos now](https://www.portland.gov/pbem/neighborhood-emergency-teams/volunteer), and PBEM will notify you when the next basic training is scheduled.

### Inner Eastside Zoning Project: Timeline Pushed Back a Year

The Portland Bureau of Planning and Sustainability has asked that the Inner Eastside Zoning Project report timeline be pushed back a year, to summer 2028, to allow time for traffic studies, community outreach, and other research. The project is exploring zoning changes that would allow a greater diversity of housing, including multi-story apartments as well as commercial buildings, across inner eastside neighborhoods. A significant portion of the Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood south of Fremont between NE 33rd and 60th avenues would be affected by these changes. Nothing has been decided, and public input will help shape the recommendations. Learn more and sign up for updates on the [City's project page](https://www.portland.gov/bps/planning/inner-eastside-zoning-project/about).
