Central NE Neighbors Crime Prevention Coordinator

When crime or other nuisance problems crop up in a neighborhood, residents may feel overwhelmed and unsure of what to do. Reactions vary from assurance to fear depending on the number of occurrences of crime in an area, the ability of the police to resolve these cases, and residents’ previous experiences with crime. That’s where crime prevention services can help. Portland’s crime prevention services are offered through the Office of Neighborhood Involvement and are designed to reduce crime, increase livability, and get neighbors involved in community policing efforts. These services are provided by Crime Prevention Coordinators (CPC) who offer problem-solving, community organizing, and education on issues of crime and public safety. They work closely with community members, public safety activists, the police, neighborhood associations, city bureaus, businesses, state agencies, school districts, and local service providers.

A typical call handled by a CPC may be from a resident concerned about a possible drug house on her street. She notices that numerous people are coming and going at odd hours. The renter/owner appears unfriendly and secretive with shades drawn at all hours. There has been an increase in theft from auto and other crimes since the occupant moved in. In this example, a CPC may provide education on illegal drug activity, how to keep a detailed log that police can use to build a case, and tips on how to prevent theft. The CPC may work with a resident to form a neighborhood watch. The CPC would be in contact with the Neighborhood Response Team Officer, a police officer working on chronic livability issues, as well as the landlord.

The mission of the Office of Neighborhood Involvement is to enhance the quality of neighborhoods through community participation. With the Crime Prevention Program, we have found that neighbors who are willing to work with us, the police, and their neighbors are able to make a profound difference by increasing the safety of their neighborhood and decreasing fear.
