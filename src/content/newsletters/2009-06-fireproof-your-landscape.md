To minimize the threat of a wildfire near your home or yard, follow these tips:

- Water plants near the house to keep fuel moisture levels high.
- Rake ground covers such as bark dust, wood chips, compost and tree litter away from combustible structures of your home (decks, siding).
- Remove nuisance plants from around homes and yard; they are highly combustible and can serve as “ladder” fuels. This includes non-native hawthorn, Himalayan blackberry, scotch broom, English holly and English ivy.

A full list of the nuisance plants that can be removed without a permit can be found on the Bureau of Planning and Sustainability site: www.portlandonline.com/planning and choose Documents and select Portland Plant List.

- Keep landscape plants free of deadwood and litter. Remove plants that are diseased or infested.
- Dispose of trimmings away from structures in a composting bin, or place them in curbside recycling containers. Do not dump light materials and lawn trimming back into natural areas. They can be an eyesore and add to the fuel load of an approaching wildfire.
- Refuel and use garden equipment carefully. Never refill a gas tank in grassy or high vegetation areas.
- If you don’t water your lawn through the summer season, or if seasonal water shortages call for temporary end to irrigation, let your lawn go dormant (turn from green to light brown) and then cut your grass as low to the ground as possible.

If you do an internet search on “Invasive Plant Management Portland or “PortlandPlant List”, you will learn more about suggested plants to plant or mitigate in your landscape plan.
