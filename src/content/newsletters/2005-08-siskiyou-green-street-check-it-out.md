The Beaumont Wilshire neighborhood has Portland’s first green street, and now they have a plaque to prove it. The City presented the plaque at the June 13 general membership meeting to commemorate the first Green Street curb extension project in the City of Portland. The plaque has photos of the green street in each season. The winter scene even has snow. The Bureau of Environmental Services also passed out postcards at the meeting with photos of the green street in different seasons.

You can find the project near the corner of NE Siskiyou and 35th Place. The City built it in 2003 to evaluate and promote ways to manage stormwater runoff using natural systems.

All of the stormwater runoff from Siskiyou Street used to flow to the combined sewer system, where it contributed to combined sewer overflows (CSOs) to the Willamette River. Now stormwater flows through the landscaped curb extension where native and ornamental plants filter pollutants, slow the flow, and let water soak into the ground.

Projects like the Siskiyou Green Street show how the City can redesign residential streets to manage stormwater effectively, stop sewer backups into basements, and reduce CSOs. Green streets also make attractive additions to neighborhoods.

Green streets are planted with low-growing vegetation adaptable to a variety of conditions. Typically they need a low level of maintenance, including removing debris, weeding, watering and replacing plants. The City is responsible for maintenance but welcomes neighborhood help with weeding or watering, especially during hot weather.

The Siskiyou green street cost about $20,000, which is more cost effective than digging up streets and installing larger sewer pipes. Green street construction is also less intrusive and takes less time. Other benefits such as cooling and filtering the air and water, creating habitat and enhancing property values make this a winning project.
