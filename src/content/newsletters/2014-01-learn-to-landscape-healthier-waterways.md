When compared to nearby forests and natural areas, urban areas have less vegetation and more roadways, parking lots, sidewalks, roof-tops, etc. These impervious surfaces prevent rainfall from soaking into the ground naturally. Instead, rainfall travels quickly over these impervious surfaces, picking up pollutants such as automobile oil and brake dust along the way. This storm water runoff typically goes untreated directly into local waterways negatively impacting water quality for people, fish and wildlife.

As part of a larger effort to bring healthy watershed function back to the neighborhood, Bureau of Environmental Services (BES) is partnering with East Multnomah Soil & Water Conservation District (EMSWCD) to host a free Rain Garden 101 and a free Naturescaping Basics workshop in the Beaumont-Wilshire community!

These workshops provide step-by-step details on how to plan, design and build your own rain garden, as well as sustainable landscaping techniques. Join your neighbors and find out how rain gardens can benefit the fish, wildlife and people that are connected to our local waterways.

In the Naturescaping Basics workshop, find out how to use native plants to create landscapes that conserve water, reduce pollution, and provide healthy habitats while saving you time, money and energy.
