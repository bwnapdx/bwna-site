Dogs can only follow your lead, and with the exception of designated dog-run areas like the one in Wilshire Park, that means leading your dog by leash. It may seem overly restrictive and unnecessary to some, but there are good reasons for keeping your dog leashed while on a walk:

Fear of others: You may have a well-trained and/or passive dog, but other dogs and humans encountered along the way won’t necessarily know that and may become fearful, especially if they’ve been bitten or chased by animals in the past. If your dog is on a leash, it’s much less likely to be a problem.

Courtesy: Many people work hard to keep their lawns and flowers looking nice. Their gardens may be as important to them as your dog is to you. Letting your dog walk through people’s flowers and yards is disrespectful and potentially harmful to what is growing there. Occasional chases of squirrels, cats, etc., onto people’s property can also be a problem. And while poop in yards and on sidewalks is by far the most common and worst offense, most dog owners do their best to promptly clean up the mess—or at least as much of it as they scoop up.

Protection: A leash allows you to keep your dog away from potentially dangerous confrontations with other animals. It’s a win-win, affording protection for the pets of others as well as your own!

Law: According to Multnomah County law: “Dogs must be properly licensed and vaccinated. Dogs must be leashed at ALL times while on public property and in parks (except in designated off-leash areas), and owners must remove their dog’s waste.” Leash and scoop laws can be enforced with fines of up to and including $150.00.

So please do the right thing. Be considerate and law-abiding. Leash up before taking that walk.
