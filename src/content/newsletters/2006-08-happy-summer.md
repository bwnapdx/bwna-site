Happy summer, Beaumont Wilshire! I am currently enjoying replacing my lawn with vegetable and flower gardens. At last year’s Bioneers conference I learned about a regional group called Food not Lawns (foodnotlawns.com) which suggests that we replace our lawns with vegetable gardens and grow our own food. For many reasons this is something I’m passionate about – it means less loud, lawn-mowers polluting the air, fresh, organic produce that I get to nurture, more birds and animal life, and money saved when I pick my own lettuce. I envision large fruit and evergreen trees on every parking strip and gardens replacing lawns. With the world running low on oil, it makes more sense for us to learn how to sustain ourselves locally. So, to those who are experimenting in this way, thank you for taking the lead and I hope you’ll share your success and learning with your neighbors as we all re-learn how to feed ourselves in the long Oregon growing season.

On another note, I have noticed how isolated many of us are from each other and am thinking of ways we might change this through street parties, traffic calming changes on Fremont, participation in our neighborhood association (we’re moving the general meetings to the Beaumont Middle School in October). Also I would like to see neighborhood rest/meeting areas. Picnic tables in parking strips could provide a place for walkers to sit and rest. This ties in nicely with one of our board member’s City Repair-style intersection project which he is bringing to his bock near Wilshire Park.

There are things I love about our community and there are things I’d like to improve. I want to live in a place where everyone feels included, especially children and elders. I will continue to share thoughts with you every two months in my President’s message and perhaps via a blog which is in the works. I would also enjoy hearing from you directly with projects or ideas you think would benefit our community – please write to me at president@bwna.org

*Albert Kaufman*
President, BWNA
