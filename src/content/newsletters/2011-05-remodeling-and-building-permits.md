Warmer weather is here, and with it comes the sounds of hammers and saws ringing through the air. Yes, home improvement season is upon us once again. Here in Beaumont-Wilshire we are lucky to live in a neighborhood of beautifully preserved older homes, most of which were built in the 1920’s, 30’s and 40’s. As a result, chances are good that if you have not already remodeled or updated certain aspects of your home, you most likely will at some point. It’s a reality of living in an older home. The most common home improvement projects in our neighborhood include remodeling of kitchens and bathrooms, conversion of basements and attics into living space, or updating of mechanical systems. All of which require permits from the City of Portland Bureau of Development Services, regardless of whether you or a contractor does the work.

This is the part where people often roll their eyes and pretend that they didn’t hear me say that last part about the permits. Obtaining the necessary permits and working thru the permit process admittedly can add time, expense, red tape, city inspections and the potential for increased frustration and headache. It also ensures that the work was performed safely, correctly, and that it meets the minimum code requirements. And it’s the law. Many people simply don’t take into consideration the long-term implications of skipping over the permit process. But I absolutely guarantee you that when you decide to cash in on all of your hard work & investment and put your home on the market, the first question a potential buyer or their agent, will ask is: “Was the lovely finished basement media room and new electrical panel installed with permits?” If the answer is no, be prepared to find that buyer, their agent as well as their lender unwilling to pay your asking price for a home with improvements that are of unknown integrity. And with good reason - improper workmanship can cause fires, floods and even structural failure. The permit process is there for a reason.

If you’re contemplating a home improvement project, it’s always a good idea to speak to someone at the Bureau of Development Services first. They offer regular Permit Nights with extended hours, geared towards homeowners, and even the opportunity to consult with a building inspector to find out exactly what is required for your specific project. Their website (www.portlandonline.com/bds) is also filled with good information and is a good resource. Happy home improving!
