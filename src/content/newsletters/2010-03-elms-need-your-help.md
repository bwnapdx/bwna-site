If you’ve strolled around the community lately, you may have noticed the sudden loss of four mature American elm trees on NE 41st, just north of Knott St., reminding us that Dutch elm disease (DED) is alive and well and seriously threatens the 30 remaining majestic elms that grace Beaumont-Wilshire streets. Many streets in older neighborhoods like ours were once lined with elms that created a continuous, high, wide canopy with myriad benefits. While healthy elms can live for several hundred years, the presence of DED means they desperately need our help if they are to survive much longer.

Experts recommend susceptible elms be pruned regularly and inoculated against DED every three years—2010 is one of those years. On June 19, volunteers with the Grant Park affiliate of Save Our Elms (SOE), a non-profit, all-volunteer organization that works to preserve mature elms and replant disease-resistant elm varieties, will inoculate the elms that form the beautiful canopy over parts of Wistaria Drive, Alameda and Beaumont Streets, and NE 41st Ave., with the assistance of City foresters.

SOE is in the midst of its fundraising drive to make the inoculations happen. The expense (an average $185 per tree, every three years) falls on residents because the city doesn’t pay for tree maintenance. Therefore, SOE needs to raise $5,550 for BW elms. Donations are greatly appreciated by April 30, so there will be time to do further fundraising, if necessary.

Volunteers are also needed. To get involved, please contact Angela at 503.281.2738 or to learn more about SOE, visit: www.saveourelms.org.
