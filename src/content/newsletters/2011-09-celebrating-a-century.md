Beulah Villa Dennis has lived her entire life within three miles of Peninsula Park. She is a true Portland native–a rare distinction in this town, made even more remarkable by the fact that Ms. Dennis just celebrated her 100th birthday.

In July, I had the pleasure of attending a birthday celebration for Ms. Dennis at Dee’s Golden Door beauty salon in the plaza at NE 42nd and Fremont.  Salon owner Nancy Tilton wanted to show her appreciation for Ms. Dennis, who has been a customer at her salon for nearly 25 years. Every other Tuesday, Ms. Dennis has her hair done at the salon by Barbara Godinez. Because she is no longer able to drive, Barbara picks Ms. Dennis up at her home and brings her to the salon. The two women have clearly developed a lasting friendship–one that goes back many years. In fact, before Barbara began working at the salon, Barbara’s mother styled Ms. Dennis’ hair. When Barbara’s mother passed away, Barbara took over.

In her career days, Ms.Dennis was a clerk for Van Duyn chocolate company and spent time working in each of their eight Portland stores. After retiring, she focused on volunteer work, donating her time to both Faubion School and Concordia University. Ms. Dennis has one daughter who lives in Salem and comes to visit her weekly. She also has three grandchildren, twelve great-grandchildren, and a variety of nieces and nephews, some of whom still live in Portland.

Over her years spent in north and northeast Portland, Ms. Dennis has seen many changes. And there are, of course, some things that haven’t changed much at all. She told me, for example, that Peninsula Park is still very much the same as it was in her teenage years, when she played tennis and swam in the pool there. She also said there once was a campground at the park, and a grocery store–both of which have long since disappeared.

Ms. Dennis told me one of her favorite parts about her routine at Dee’s Golden Door is the social interactions it brings her. She also said they do a great manicure. My sense is that she’s a bit of a celebrity; in the short time I was at the salon, many people stopped by to wish her a happy birthday. It’s clear that Ms. Dennis is an important and cherished part of her community and I’m fortunate to have met her.
