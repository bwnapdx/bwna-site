Of the Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association’s (BWNA) five general meetings held during the year, the one that inevitably stands out above the rest, “The Taste of Beaumont”— held on the second Monday of April— and this year’s event on April 11th was one for the record books.

First, there was the overflow crowd (upwards of 90) packed into Bethany Lutheran Church’s Fellowship Hall, heartily dining on cuisine samples provided free of charge by neighborhood eating establishments. Lively music was provided by the talented young musicians of the Beaumont Middle School Small Jazz Combo under the direction of community favorite Cynthia Plank. Then came the ambitious business meeting agenda, with upwards of a dozen topics and speakers in little more than an hour, including the need to fill a vacated Treasurer’s position and four At Large Board of Directors positions—not to mention a culminating door prize raffle!

Incredibly, everything got done on time…and with stellar results. Speaking first was Officer Wayne Schull, who assured residents that despite recent reports of car prowl activity, the Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood’s overall crime rate remains one of the lowest in the area. He went on to answer questions and encouraged residents to keep him informed. Next came presentations on BWNA finances, the reconfigured Newsletter team, and the refurbished BWNA Web site (now with blog & Facebook page). Finally, it was time for elections, as nominees were sought to fill vacant positions. And for the first time in years, all BWNA positions were filled! Board member Gwen MacPherson was elected as Treasurer, with BW residents Serah Breakstone, Larry Freeborg, Michael Gray, and Alex Wall stepping forward to fill the vacant At Large Board positions. Board members reelected to another two-year term were Vice President Bill Markwart, Secretary Jim Karlock, and At Large Board members Dave Anderson, Colin O’Neill, and Michael Rounds.

The meeting also featured presentations on a wide variety of subjects: the upcoming all-neighborhood graffiti cleanup, the Movies in the Park Festival, the National Night Out Picnic, Fremont Fest, the Portland School District bond measures up for a vote in mid-May, Beaumont Middle School Earth Day campus beautification, Susie’s Forest project, and emergency preparedness from Neighborhood Emergency Team leader Liz Johnston. The evening was capped by the raffling off of ten beautiful floral arrangements courtesy of Beaumont Florist and two $25 gift certificates courtesy of Alameda Brewhouse.
