Whether it’s the electric twang of the Byrd’s top-ten hit of the ‘60s or the original grassroots folksong version by Pete Seeger, or the timeless (no pun intended) plaintive soliloquy of a frustrated King Solomon in Ecclesiastes, “a time to” message has resonated down the years in the hearts and minds of millions of people for different reasons and in different ways. And so it was for me not long ago. Unexpectedly, as I sat contemplating BWNA priorities for the coming months, the melody and lyrics to “Turn, Turn, Turn” began echoing in my head! Meditating on the beauty of the song and wisdom in the words, I wondered if something in this venerable passage could be applied to my task at hand--namely, the charting of a course for BWNA--and immediately one line jumped out at me: “A time to sow…”

The analogies are pretty straightforward: Just as the farmer spreads out seeds, hoping that they will germinate into bountiful crops, so the neighborhood association disseminates funding and volunteers, hoping that the projects and activities they support  will enhance and improve the livability of the neighborhood. Similarly, just as the farmer must determine what seeds make most sense to plant at which times of the year, so the neighborhood association must prioritize its projects and activities and decide how and when to implement them.

Upon reflection, it occurred to me that the October-November time period affords a golden opportunity for BWNA “to sow;” i.e., to begin its outreach into the community. Sandwiched between the summer vacation months--with their accompanying get-away-from-it-all  mindset--and the winter holiday months--with their emphasis on gift-shopping and family visits--the months of October and November seem ripe for shifting our attention outwardly to public service efforts.

But where to start? Well, like the farmer, it’s wise to look back on what you’ve accomplished in the past before proceeding into the future. This past year the Association was effective in helping to persuade TriMet not to discontinue the Fremont-33 bus  line, in assisting Beaumont Middle School with the purchase of new white boards, in working cooperatively with Beaumont Middle School students and parents on an environmentally-friendly campus beautification project,  in montoring coyote activity in the neighborhood and publicizing advice from wild life experts, and in taking steps to mitigate traffic safety/calming problems, such as the infamous “Wistaria curve” in the 41st and Wistaria area. The Association was also actively  involved this summer in organizing and participating in an outdoor gathering at Beaumont Middle School in praise and support of Portland State student and neighborhood resident Bao Vuong, who was the victim of a hate crime in broad daylight on Fremont Street. Bao set an example by having the courage to share his experience for others to learn from, and our neighborhood backed him 100%! These are the kinds of achievements that demonstrate the good that can come from an engaged neighborhood association.

So, now is the time for us to sow--time to decide what programs to fund, what projects to pursue. An educator by profession, my personal priority will be to continue BWNA’s longstanding partnership with our neighborhood’s educational hub, Beaumont Middle School, working closely with new Principal Liz Casson-Taylor to strengthen facilities and programs.  That’s one priority.

Ask yourself what your priority would be for the neighborhood. Then act on it. Make your way down the walkway to Fellowship Hall at Bethany Lutheran Church on the second Monday of the month, express your ideas at our General Meeting, and  contribute to the health of the neighborhood.

After all, it’s a time of the season for community involvement! Turn, turn, turn.
