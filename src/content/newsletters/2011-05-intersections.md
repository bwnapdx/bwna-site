A vestige of the Wild West remains on several streets in the Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood and some residents want that to change.

Uncontrolled intersections – those without stop signs or other traffic control devices – are a constant worry, particularly in the area south of Fremont Street and east of 41st Avenue. John McLaren, who lives at Northeast 46th Avenue and Siskiyou Street, has seen four accidents in 11 years. In the worst, a young woman crashed into his sun room and tore out a section of fence.

“It’s a concern of mine,” McLaren said. “No one seems to know the law and they’re going way too fast. It’s amazing to me there aren’t more accidents.”

But the fact is there’s no identified safety problem, said Scott Batson, a traffic engineer with the city’s Transportation Bureau. City transportation experts believe that uncertainty caused by the unmarked intersections causes drivers to be more cautious.

According to city records, there has only been one crash at 46th and Siskiyou from 2005 to 2009, the most recent data. During that same time, there were three reported accidents at Klickitat and 46th, one at 45th and Klickitat, and four at Siskiyou and 45th, Batson said.

The city’s threshold for considering a two-way stop is three reported crashes that could have been prevented with stop signs within a three-year period, said Eileen Dent of the Transportation Bureau’s traffic investigations unit. Restricted views can also be a reason for adding a two-way stop.

One consideration is expense. It costs about $600 to put in a two-way stop, Batson said. In addition, there’s evidence that drivers speed up between stop signs and the additional stopping and starting increases pollution. Stop signs also aren’t effective at reducing speeding and some drivers will simply ignore them.

Rebecca Galloway, who lives at 46th and Klickitat, worries that businesses along Fremont are attracting drivers who are unfamiliar with the neighborhood and aren’t aware some intersections are uncontrolled. The good news is that Klickitat Street will change later this year when it’s converted to a bike boulevard. Except at major intersections such as 41st Avenue, stop signs will be put in at the numbered cross streets to allow bike traffic to flow along Klickitat.

Not all residents want stop signs. Kevin Parsons, who lives at 45th and Siskiyou, said he remembers only one accident in the 13 years he’s been there and is OK with the uncontrolled intersection.

But those who get stop signs generally say the situation improves. About a year ago, the city added stop signs to uncontrolled intersections in the neighborhood west of 33rd Avenue, near Alameda Elementary. That was because only eight intersections out of about 36 in that area were uncontrolled and it completed a neighborhood stop plan, Batson said. If a majority of intersections have stop signs, the city may add two-way stops to the remaining intersections to avoid confusion.

Andrea Haack lives at one of those intersections, Northeast 29th Avenue and Siskiyou Street, and appreciated the two-way stop.

“I always prefer stop signs because people are crazy without them,” Haack said. “Everyone seems so nervous or they’re not nervous and they’re jerks.”

Ted Farthing, who also lives at the 29th and Siskiyou intersection, moved to Portland from New York and was astonished to find uncontrolled intersections, which he called a leftover from frontier days. He takes issue with the city’s contention that uncontrolled intersections make drivers more cautious.

“That’s ridiculous,” Farthing said.

For the record, the Oregon Driver Manual requires drivers to look and yield the right of way to any vehicle in the intersection or approaching from your right at the same time.

Residents who want to request a stop sign may call the city at 503.823.SAFE (7233).
