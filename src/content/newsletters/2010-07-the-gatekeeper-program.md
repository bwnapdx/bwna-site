The Gatekeeper Program, developed in 1978 at Spokane Mental Health’s Elder Services and implemented in Multnomah County in 1987, has been applied nationally and internationally to train the community to identify and refer isolated, at-risk older adults residing in their own homes.

Social services have typically relied on traditional methods to inform people about available services and the expectation is that those in need will call and ask for help. Such efforts have not been successful in reaching those most isolated in the community. There are a number of reasons why high-risk older adults are unable, unwilling or resistant to seeking assistance; negative stigma associated with using “social services”, pride, fear of losing control over one’s life, shame, financial reasons, mental health issues (paranoia, memory loss, depression) and lack of information.

Who are Gatekeepers? Gatekeepers are employees of local businesses, friends and neighbors who come into contact with older adults on a regular basis. Through the Gatekeeper Program at Multnomah County Aging and Disability Services, Gatekeepers receive a brief training on how to recognize “red flags” that indicate a customer or community member may be in need of assistance and at risk for hospitalization and/or premature out of home placement. They will also learn the steps to take in referring such an individual to the Gatekeeper Program. Listening and Observing are the key elements in identifying those who may be experiencing difficulties and may be at risk. The Gatekeeper’s willingness to be aware and alert while in the presence of an older person or an adult with disabilities is what makes the program work. By recognizing “red flags” and making a call to report the issues, Gatekeepers may be saving a life!

The role of the Gatekeeper is not to be a social worker or mental health professional, but rather to purely recognize signs and symptoms that may indicate an older adult or adult with disabilities is in need of assistance or support and then to make the referral to the proper authorities—in this case, the Gatekeeper Referral Line at Multnomah County Aging and Disability Services.

Without Gatekeepers many individuals would never receive necessary assistance and their independence, health and possible life would be in jeopardy. To date, 48% of all Gatekeeper referrals received are people who are currently NOT receiving any support or services—so they truly are isolated in their homes. Also, 63% of the referrals received are issues categorized as “abuse”, “neglect” or “exploitation”!
