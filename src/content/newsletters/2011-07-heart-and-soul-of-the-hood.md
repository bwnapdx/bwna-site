What would you say constitutes the “heart and soul” of the Beaumont-Wilshire (BW) neighborhood? A particular area or institution, attitude or lifestyle? It all depends on your definition of “heart and soul” as it relates to our neighborhood. My preference is to separate “heart” and “soul” into two distinct descriptors—“heart” as the most centrally-located hub of activity and “soul” as the embodiment of our community spirit and ambience. Given this approach, what area or aspect of the neighborhood would you choose for each?

For me, the choices are obvious:

“Heart” = the intersection of Fremont Street & 42nd Avenue • “Soul” = Wilshire Park

Why? If a Portlander is unfamiliar with Beaumont-Wilshire, just mention “NE Fremont & 42nd, next to Beaumont Middle School”— and problem solved. It’s the closest thing we have to a welcoming gateway to all who enter the neighborhood.

And Wilshire Park? Where else so uniquely captures the character and personality of BW residents? Just have a seat and observe — dog lovers, baby strollers, physical fitness enthusiasts, walkers, bicyclists, kids at play, picnickers, people of all ages, races, etc. That’s the lifestyle we embrace; that’s why we choose to live here.

But a heart weakens without care, and a soul dims without love. Fremont Street, 42nd Avenue, and Wilshire Park would not be the successes they are today without people who make it a priority in their lives to help improve and enhance the community. Some do it by becoming active in Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association (BWNA), Beaumont Business Association, or NE 42nd Avenue Business Association. Others contribute through their ties with Beaumont Middle School. But mostly, it’s accomplished by neighbors, both individually and collectively, who continually take the initiative to make living in this neighborhood better for us all.

Take for example the BW Graffiti Cleanup last May, soon to be followed by a second wave of cleaning on July 30th. While our neighborhood is considered “cleaner than most” by the city’s Graffiti Abatement staff, taggings and stickers on signs, fences, and buildings continue to mar the landscape—a seemingly never-ending assault by mean-spirited vandals and mindless pranksters. Nonetheless, neighbors are willing to get up early on a Saturday morning and team up with the Graffiti Abatement pros to fight back!

We also can do our parts to maintain livability by simply “doing the right thing” and encouraging others to do the same. For instance, after completing the cleaning of graffiti from the mural in the Wilshire Park pavilion, Graffiti Abatement Coordinator Marcia Dennis commented on how dirty the mural wall had become: “Why they think it’s appropriate to use a mural as a backboard for balls when the opposite wall is blank I’ll never understand.” Who are the “they” referred to by Marcia? Probably just unthinking kids having fun. But if we see it happening, let’s speak up and set them on the right path.

Same goes with litter—especially in and around the park. If we notice a pile of stuff that has been dumped on the grass or by the curb, put on your gloves, grab a sack, and get it picked up! No, it’s not our fault when uncaring slobs despoil the environs, but it is our responsibility to act as good stewards in maintaining the natural beauty around us. So, have fun in the sun, but do take heart to preserve the soul of our ‘hood.
