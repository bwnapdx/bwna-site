Do you want to be a better speaker? Develop your leadership skills? Meet some new friends? Toastmasters clubs are places to do all three. Columbia Center Toastmasters meets Saturday mornings from 9:00-10:15 a.m. in the chapel at Rose City Park United Methodist Church, 5830 NE Alameda. http://columbiacenter.freetoasthost.us/index.html

A number of Beaumont Wilshire residents belong to the club, and visitors are welcome at any meeting.

I first joined Toastmasters five years ago, not long after I moved to the neighborhood from Massachusetts. I wanted to meet some people as well as continuing to develop my speaking skills.

As a minister who works with clergy who want to be better leaders and churches who want to develop their ministries, I do a lot of speaking. And for many years I preached a sermon every Sunday. But I knew I can get better. I prepare for upcoming talks by practicing at Toastmasters. If the group laughs at a joke, I figure it’s safe to tell to another audience. If they tell me they were a little confused by a section of a speech, I know I need to work harder to make myself clear.

A central part of every Toastmasters meeting is evaluation. Everyone who speaks gets a kind but helpful evaluation. “I liked the way you did this, and here is something you might work on,” is the usual way comments are given. I appreciate the feedback I get at Toastmasters, and it helps me improve.

Do you have to be an experienced speaker to benefit from Toastmasters? No, not at all. We’ve had members who have never been in front of a group in their life. Over time, they become more comfortable and less nervous, and begin to feel they can be themselves even in front of an audience. One of my biggest thrills as a Toastmaster is watching people grow as speakers, and become more confident than they ever thought possible.
