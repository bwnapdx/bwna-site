Spring seems especially welcome this year. One has only to think back a few months ago to our shoveling, slipping, and sliding during a record snowfall--and the numerous dark, chilly days that followed--to appreciate the increasingly longer daylight hours, warmer temperatures, and colorful blossoms coming our way. It lifts the spirits, and everything seems more hopeful. In fact, this is the perfect time to reflect on and be thankful for the positives in our lives--like the neighborhood we live in, for instance!

These thoughts were imprinted on my mind after attending the first meeting of BWNA’s newly-constituted Newsletter Team. Editor Dan Johnson had organized the gathering at his house, and his living room was full of volunteers--some Newsletter veterans, some first-timers. As we became acquainted and got into discussions, I marveled at the diversity of personalities, talents, and skills on display. Many of these people had lived in the neighborhood for decades--some even growing up nearby--and one had just recently moved here from another state. To a person, they echoed each other on the good fortune of living where they do and took pride in contributing to the Newsletter--writing articles, assisting with production, and distributing copies to our porches every other month. The “distributors” were particularly enthusiastic about their role, citing both their enjoyment in getting to know the neighborhood on a house-to-house basis and the walking exercise involved. What struck me about everyone on the team was not just how much they loved the neighborhood and its environs, but also, and particularly, the pleasure they derived from actively contributing to it. (By the way, Newsletter Distributor Director Marsha Mahon is on the lookout for a couple more volunteers to be distributors on her team. If you’re interested, please give her a call at 503.284.1859.)

So, put your worries aside and celebrate the new season by taking a walk around the park, along 42nd, up and down Fremont, in and around side streets--all the while keeping in mind how great it is to call this place home. Better yet, bring yourself and your family to the most entertaining and tastiest BWNA General Meeting of the year--”A Taste of Beaumont”--featuring free cuisine samples from some of your favorite neighborhood restaurants and music from the Beaumont Middle School band (see article on page one). And while you’re at it, consider volunteering to become actively involved in BWNA--as a Board member, on the Newsletter Team, or as a member of a committee.

There’s no better time of the year to begin contributing to the well-being of this neighborhood.

Al Ellis
