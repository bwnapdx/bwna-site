Hurricane Katrina taught countless lessons in disaster preparedness. Among them, we learned just how much our pets depend on us for their safety and well-being. During an emergency, the best way to protect your pets is to include them in your family’s disaster plan.

We’ve seen how animals left behind in a disaster can easily be injured, lost or killed. If you need to evacuate your home, try to take your pets with you. It might be difficult to find shelter for your animals during a crisis (Red Cross shelters, for example, cannot take pets due to health regulations), so plan ahead by creating a list of pet-friendly hotels that are within driving distance.

In case you’re not home during a disaster, find a trusted neighbor who would be willing to take your pets and meet you at a pre-arranged location if possible. This person should be comfortable with your pets, know where your pet disaster supply kit is kept, and have a key to your home.

Pet supplies for your family’s 72-hour emergency kit include:

- Portable carrier (essential for cats)
- Leash or harness
- Food and water bowls
- Three-day supply of food and water
- Litter and litter box for cats
- Medications
- First aid kit
- Current photos of your pet in case he/she gets lost
- Health records, including vaccination dates
- Lists of pet-friendly hotels and phone numbers
- Instructions on feeding schedule, medications and any special needs

Lastly, make sure your dog or cat always wears a securely fastened collar with ID tags so that up-to-date information is readily available if they ever get lost—whether there’s a disaster or not.
