## Write a Letter to the Board

The Board of Directors welcomes letters concerning local neighborhood issues. Writers are asked to limit letters to 150 words. Letters may be edited for length and clarity and will be published as space allows, including on our website/blog (www.bwna.us).

## Letter to the Board

September 28, 2011

Letter to the Board:

As neighbors that live adjacent to the island at the intersection of NE Klickitat and Alameda, we have a vested interest in the plans for the island and surrounding streets. The current plan design (60% complete) expands the width and length of the island, adds a rain garden on the North side and narrows both streets at the intersection of NE Klickitat and Alameda.

Many of our neighbors have given feedback along the way and have been in contact with the City. Whereas everyone has been very professional and willing to listen and discuss our concerns, we are certain the project will go forward. Our concerns are:

1. Narrowing of NE Klickitat and Alameda at the intersection. This will lessen the amount of street parking for several residences in the area and will increase congestion on a busy street. Alameda is one of Portland’s wide residential streets and this change will impact the aesthetics of this iconic street.

2. Maintenance of the island. Currently, and for several years neighbors have maintained the island. We designed and paid for the plantings, mowed the lawn weekly, edged and maintained the beds. The neighbors took this on because the City stopped maintaining the island. Recently the neighbors were given access to the key to turn the water on. Water is paid for privately – not by the city. Now, what can we expect when the rain garden is planted? We are being told that the City will maintain the rain garden side of the island, but not the whole island. If we have the money to spend on this project, why are we not planning for the whole island to be maintained by the City?

3. We also have been told that the rain garden will cost approximately $100,000 to build. What is the return on that investment? Given the financial state of affairs, do we have money to do this? Is there a less expensive option?

We would like the City to accomplish the rain garden without impacting the width of the streets involved.

Roger & Cynthia Gahlsdorf

Mark & Kris Olson

John Spathas
