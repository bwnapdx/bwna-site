## Lights Out: Take Action to Help Protect Birds this Fall!

The Bird Alliance of Oregon reports that many millions of birds will fly over Oregon this fall on their way to their wintering grounds. You can help keep them safe by turning off any non-essential lights from 11 p.m. to dawn during the critical peak migration period between September 19 and October 19. And that's not all! Consider making this a nightly habit year round to reduce impacts on migrating or nesting birds as well as fish, amphibians, mammals, insects, and human health! For more information, visit bit.ly/Lights-Out-Oregon. A nod to Kepper Petzing of Concordia News for forwarding this info to BWNA!

## Pacific Holiday Turns 5

Consignment store Pacific Holiday at 4443 NE Fremont St. celebrates its fifth anniversary in September. This is an important milestone; according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 45% of businesses fail before their fifth anniversary. And in Pacific Holiday's case, it was necessary to navigate during a pandemic. Hats off to owner Allyson Medeles, her crew, and the consignors who share their pre-loved clothing and accessories!

## Slow Down on NE 42nd Avenue

The Portland Bureau of Transportation will reduce the speed limit on NE 42nd Avenue to 20 mph from NE Prescott Street to NE Killingsworth Street. The new speed limit supports the City of Portland's Vision Zero goal to eliminate traffic deaths and serious injuries on our streets. Speed is a factor in nearly half of all deadly crashes that occur in Portland. At lower speeds, it is easier to avoid crashes altogether. And if a crash occurs, it is less likely people are killed or seriously injured. Visit portland.gov/transportation/vision-zero for more info.

## Sober Living Center to Open at 42nd and Prescott

BWNA earlier reported on the Society 42 building, a sort of "communal" living development with 44 bedrooms at the southwest corner of NE 42nd and Prescott Street. Recently completed, tenancy in its entirety will be by the non-profit 4D Recovery for use as a sober living center. Residents will move in sometime in late fall.

Tony Vezina, 4D's executive director, explained that 4D has over a decade of experience helping people find sobriety and overcome alcohol and drug addiction; securing the Society 42 building for its use is the organization's latest effort to achieve this community goal.

"The program will support people exiting drug treatment who want to continue their sobriety with the help of recovery mentors and a sober environment," said Vezina. "Participants must graduate treatment before entering the program, agree to be sober while in the program, and act as good neighbors during their stay."

Vezina welcomes contact if there are concerns. For now, email support@4DRecovery.org; more contact information will be made available as hiring for the center comes to completion.
