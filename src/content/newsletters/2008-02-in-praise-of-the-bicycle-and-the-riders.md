Michael and Yoshiko Kamata Bacon have much in common with their neighbors. They own their 1922 home. Both work full time. Michael is Portland School District’s Immersion Education Coordinator, a job that takes him to 18 different schools in the metropolitan area. Yoshiko is a pre-school teacher at Richmond Elementary School in southeast Portland. They are parents to six-year old Keita and four-year old Hazuki. Keita plays soccer and takes art lessons. His sister Hazuki studies dance. In addition, Michael is taking courses through Portland State University to qualify for an administrative license.

Recently, I sat down with Michael and Yoshiko to talk about their decision to sell the family minivan and commit to bicycling as their primary mode of transportation. My questions were:

What were you thinking? They had been thinking about the decision for a long time. They moved into the neighborhood eleven years ago because of nearby services and public transportation. They wanted to reduce the negative environmental impact of driving a car and realize financial savings. So, almost three years ago, they sold their car and bought one new bicycle.

What does a typical day look like? On weekdays, Yoshiko leaves the house by bicycle at 6:30 a.m. to begin her day. Michael gets the kids ready, hooks up the tag-along and/or trailer to transport the kids to the school bus pickup. Sometimes Keita rides his own bike to the school bus. Once safely aboard the bus, Michael either locks the extra gear at a bicycle park, or turns around and brings it home. Next, he rides to the bus stop and catches a lift to his office in North Portland (and probably to one or more of the schools with an immersion program). At the end of her day, Yoshiko travels Michael’s journey in reverse.

How have your lives changed? Both Yoshiko and Michael feel that their choice has forced them to slow down and simplify their lives. They spend more time outdoors and walking. They experience and appreciate nature. They plan their outings more carefully and efficiently. And they feel more connected to the neighborhood and community.

What have been some challenges? When one or both parents are sick and don’t feel like riding their bicycles. When Keita broke a bone in his elbow twice in the same year. When Michael works all day and has evening classes in Milwaukie. When friends and family question their judgment.

Are you happy with your decision? The Bacons believe they have made the right choice for them. They believe the challenges provide an opportunity to problem solve. Even when Keita broke a bone, they were able to get timely transportation help from neighbors. And, in a pinch, they have a FlexCar membership to fall back on when they need to leave town, have out-of-town guests, or even just to take a rare break from the routine.

All in all, things work smoothly; and all four family members seem satisfied with their lifestyle. In fact, when I asked six-year old Keita what he likes best about riding his bike, he responded: “Riding with no hands, jumping, and doing tricks”. Sounds like fun!
