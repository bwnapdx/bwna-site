For many, "dog days" has a negative connotation. Although having its origin in Greek astrology, the modern interpretation conjures up a picture of panting dogs and perspiring humans under an oppressive August sun. But in Portland--with its notorious reputation for overcast and rain--the arrival of sun-filled, low humidity dog days is cause for celebration, not exasperation. And nowhere is that more true than in our own Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood. Take a stroll and see for yourself.

On the sidewalks, you will delight to the abundance of energetic moms & dads pushing strollers & exercising dogs. On the perimeters of streets, marvel at the wide array of joggers, walkers, and bicyclists. In the park, check out the fraternity of dogs frolicking in the unleashed dog area, the happy toddlers on play equipment, excited athletes and fans at the ball fields, and, of course, picnickers throughout. Conclude your stroll along 41st, 42nd, and Fremont--surrounded by patrons at sidewalk cafes, restaurants, specialty shops, massage therapy centers, etc.--and you've taken the pulse of a neighborhood that is arguably at its finest during the dog days of summer.

Highlighting the season are two Beaumont-Wilshire blockbuster events that attract the neighborhood's largest crowds of the year and are only days apart: **Fremont Fest** and the **National Night Out Beaumont-Wilshire Picnic.** With the expanding commercial growth on Fremont Street, the Beaumont-Wilshire Business Association-sponsored **Fremont Fest (Saturday, Aug. 1st)** has blossomed into one of Portland's premier summer street festivals. And the **National Night Out Beaumont-Wilshire Picnic** in Wilshire Park (Tuesday, Aug. 4th) annually ranks among the best attended National Night Out community picnics in the city--with Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association (providing burgers, hotdogs, drinks, & entertainment and neighbors preparing an assortment of potluck appetizers, salads, entrees, and desserts. Longtime residents will tell you that these two gala celebrations are not to be missed!

So you see, dog days are happy times around the Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood, not only because of the fun activities and events themselves, but also because of the opportunities neighbors have to interact with one another and collectively appreciate the totality of their environment--one in which both humans and dogs revel in the warmth of the sun. Enjoy!
