## President's Message

Ancient Athenian philosopher Socrates maintained that the unexamined life is not worth living—an extreme credo to be sure, but one that eventually formed the basis for modern scientific inquiry. Not to mention the New Year's tradition of resolutions—our annual reexamination of life's purposes and priorities, when we vow to learn from mistakes and make good on unfulfilled promises. But Socrates would undoubtedly have questioned the wisdom of limiting such personal introspection to a yearly ritual; indeed, he was an advocate of continually investigating every aspect of existence, seeking answers and insights through the use of probing questions—a learning technique now referred to as the Socratic Method.

So consider the following Socratic-inspired supposition: the unexamined neighborhood association is not worth convening. A bit harsh, perhaps? No doubt. (In fact, one could easily conclude that the statement is downright draconian!) Nonetheless, periodic questioning of BWNA's purposes and priorities has, on balance, benefitted both the organization and the neighborhood as a whole. With that in mind, and in the spirit of the Socratic Method, let's do a little probing:

**Question #1**: Practically speaking, what can BWNA achieve for its residents?

Answer: Communication and organization. In terms of communication, BWNA meetings and events provide opportunities for neighbors to socialize and touch base on issues of common interest. Additionally, the BWNA Web site & blog (www.bwna.us), e-mail lists, and newsletter help keep residents informed of local happenings and aware of each other's needs and concerns. With respect to organization, BWNA has a strong track record of advocating at City Hall and elsewhere for programs and ordinances that enhance the livability of our neighborhood—from traffic calming projects (like the installation of speed bumps on Wistaria Drive) to opposition to a residential cell tower to changes in building codes to require on-site parking for large apartment buildings.

**Question #2:** How is BWNA limited in what it can achieve for its residents?

Answer: Neighborhood associations are prohibited from endorsing ballot measures or candidates. Nor can they become involved in legal proceedings. And votes taken at a general meeting are advisory, with final approval needed from the Board of Directors. Lastly, BWNA has no tax-exempt nonprofit status in and of itself; that legal umbrella is provided by Central NE Neighbors.

**Question #3:** What are BWNA's priorities for the coming year?

Answer: A major priority for 2014 will be to fill vacancies—two on the Board and at least two reporters/writers for the Newsletter. Newsletter route deliverers are also needed.

At the heart of BWNA's success are its volunteers, especially the Board and Newsletter staff. Without these dedicated Good Samaritans, BWNA doesn't function.

Other priorities? You tell us. It's your neighborhood, your opportunity to influence BWNA's priorities & purposes. Resolve to become an active participant in BWNA!
