Art has always been big in my life. My grandfather drew cartoon figures for us as children. My favorite classes in school were art and literature. I continued to study art, at times seriously—a degree in Humanities, at times casually—classes when I had time, as I worked and enjoyed much creative freedom cutting hair. But, finally I knew I must set up a studio and work every day at art.

In February 2003, I moved with my partner, Barry Frankel, to the Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood. We settled in, did some renovations and I set up a studio in the basement. Barry is a photographer and we often spend a day out shooting and sketching the landscape and people around Portland. I like ink for sketching. I can then either do a watercolor wash over the drawing or transfer it to larger watercolor paper and paint.

I do more watercolors but like oils as well. They offer different opportunities, both of which I enjoy. A style is hard to define. I may start with reality and bend it into an abstraction such as, “Abstract Flower Black” or start with random brush strokes and find a shape and expand it into a version of reality, for example “Eyes”. I do not aim for reality, my goal is to express a feeling and hopefully evoke a feeling in the viewer.

I appreciate being part of the Beaumont-Wilshire artspace because I can tell friends, family and potential buyers where to see a sampling of my work. I will soon have an individual on-line portfolio to expand that access. I enjoy seeing what the other contributors are doing. It is satisfying to know we live in an area with so much creative energy.
