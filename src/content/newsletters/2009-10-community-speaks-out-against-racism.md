In the beginning of August, just around the time of Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association’s (BWNA) National Night-Out Picnic, dedicated to neighbors working together to prevent crime, Association President Al Ellis was informed of a racially-motivated attack on a Beaumont-Wilshire resident in the heart of the neighborhood in broad daylight. Bao Vuong, a Portland State graduate student was in his car on Fremont Street across from Bagel Land, when a group of angry youths began shouting racial insults at him and charged his car. A witness called 911, but by the time the police arrived the assailants had fled the scene and could not be located. The investigation is ongoing.

Not physically harmed, but emotionally shaken, Bao, who is the current President of the PSU Vietnamese Student Association, made the decision to go public with his story to community organizations, local officials, and the press. The response was rapid and widespread, with local media showcasing the incident. In an effort to address the situation in a proactive and constructive manner, BWNA teamed with Central Northeast Neighbors (CNN) and Reverend Joseph Santos-Lyons, head of the Community Development Program for IRCO (The Immigrant and Refugee Community Organization) to organize a “Community Speakout”, not only as a neighborhood show of support for Bao, but, more broadly, as a forum for condemnation of racist activity anywhere in our community.

Held in the Beaumont Middle School parking lot adjacent to Fremont, a crowd comprised of local residents and representatives from various civic and ethnic groups gathered together on Friday, August 7th to hear from CNN Executive Director Alison Stoll, BWNA President Al Ellis, City Commissioner Nick Fish, County Commissioner Jeff Cogan, State Representative Michael Dembrow, The City of Portland’s Office of Human Relations, representatives from the Urban League and Asian Pacific American Network of Oregon (APANO), and, of course, from Bao himself. Reported by local media, the speeches repeatedly emphasized two basic points: moral indignation that such an attack could happen in our community and the importance of speaking out about racist acts, both personally and collectively.

So what began as a shock to the normally quiet and friendly Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood culminated in a positive statement of community solidarity, with residents united as one against hate crimes. The event was given a festive touch as well, with the Rose City Park Neighborhood Association graciously providing hot dogs, hamburgers, and chips for everyone to enjoy.
