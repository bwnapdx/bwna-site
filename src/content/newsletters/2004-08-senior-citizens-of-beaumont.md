With the warm weather, everyone is outside and this provides a wonderful opportunity to meet your neighbors. Many of them are older, some live alone, and many do not trust people coming to their doors. Therefore, take advantage of the nice weather when more people are out to get to know your senior neighbors. You may notice that some of them are more active than some younger people: they run, walk, bicycle. Others need the extra support of their neighbors, relatives, and friends to get around.

For some, friends and family are nearby and provide the daily contact that they need. Some elderly folks are alone and don’t have a strong support network. Find out what your elder neighbors need, what you can do to make their lives a bit easier, and how you can help in keeping them independent. You and your children can learn great things by taking the time to listen to the stories about their past, and they can benefit from the personal contact with someone they trust. Elderly people should have contact with others daily.

It may be hard for some to accept the outside help. Those of us who have parents in other cities or states, certainly hope that they have the help and support that they need. We know that our frequent phone calls do not replace the face-to-face contact that people require.

Make a special effort to keep an eye on the elders in your neighborhood especially as we hear that the number of car prowls and break-ins increase during the summer months. Watch for strangers approaching your neighbors’ doors and find out what they are up to, as elders can be targets for scams.

There are many support networks for seniors; one is the Neighborhood Haus (www.nhweb.org; 503-246-1663 ext 113). They provide or link people to services such as friendly visitors, professional services-home visitation, senior classes, transportation aide and other services. There is also the Hollywood Senior Center at 1820 NE 40th, which offers activities, classes and services. (503-721-6777). And see the article about volunteering as a driver for Ride Connection on p. 4. We can all help out.
