Whether it’s attending outdoor picnics and concerts in Wilshire Park, strolling the Fremont Festival,or simply chatting with neighbors while gardening, summertime brings us closer together as neighbors--engendering a kind of laid-back sense of community. Life slows down a bit during the “dog days,” and opportunities abound for interacting with each other--albeit with the focus often on the lighter side of life. We make family (and pet) introductions, share anecdotes and jokes, and muse about how long it’s been since the last rainfall. In effect, a “time out” is declared from the controversies and contentions of the day. This conjures up fond college memories of Hawaiian classmates who, grinning ear to ear, would gesture with a wiggle of the thumb and pinky finger and reassure us that everything was OK: “No big deal, Bro!”

Summer is a time of minimizing worry and maximizing enjoyment. But it’s also an ideal season for meetings and greetings--be it sidewalk, yard, or park--and, from Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association’s (BWNA) point of view, the perfect occasion for collectively discussing issues of importance around the neighborhood. Accordingly, each summer the BWNA Board of Directors reevaluates goals and objectives in its Action Plan (required of each neighborhood association in the Central Northeast Neighbors coalition--CNN), with community input an essential factor in determining what the neighborhood’s priorities should be and how best to achieve them.

The fact is that BWNA is most effective at helping to enhance the livability of the neighborhood when it works in coordination with groups of concerned residents on a particular issue--like, for instance, on the recent “cell tower” issue. Regardless of your personal view on the placing of a tower and transmitting equipment in a residential zone of the BW neighborhood, one has to be impressed with how quickly and efficiently nearby residents organized themselves--forming “RespectPDX”--to do research, disseminate information, and collaborate with BWNA in the drafting and passing of a resolution in opposition to Clearwire’s proposed project. Not only did this joint effort successfully galvanize support within the Beaumont-Wilshire (BW) neighborhood, but the message resonated throughout Portland via media and City Commissioner concern. The BWNA resolution would eventually be endorsed by the seven other neighborhood associations in the CNN coalition, as well as the N.E. 42nd Ave. Business Association; and along the way, RespectPDX leader Colin O’Neill became BWNA’s newest Board member (see page six).

Hence, the mouse can roar, but it doesn’t happen without initiative. The process begins with a gathering of neighbors--perhaps at the August 3rd National Night Out Picnic at Wilshire Park--and the sharing of ideas and concerns with one another. Once organized to accomplish a specific goal, residents can make their case to BWNA and elicit Association support in persuading the Powers That Be to the desired outcome; and what better time to get the ball rolling on an issue of importance to you and your neighbors than during this glorious outdoor season!
