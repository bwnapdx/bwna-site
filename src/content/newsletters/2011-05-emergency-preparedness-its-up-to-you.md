An editorial in the March 20, 2011, Oregonian, in reference to the Japan earthquake disaster partially stated: “It’s up to each of us to stow away water and food, to know how to shut off the gas, to agree in calm conversations where we’ll meet up to hunker down, wait things out – and lend each other a helping hand.”

A group of about 15 Neighborhood Emergency Team (NET) members have been active for many years since taking the Portland Office of Emergency Management’s NET training. Through the course of our team meetings and discussions, our training has taught us to take care of ourselves first, then family and then community.

Experts continually advise us to do the simple things this editorial refers to. But have we? Is it really necessary? We know for a fact that for a period of time immediately following a major disaster, emergency services are overwhelmed. The more you can be individually prepared, the better you can sustain yourselves until resources are available and restored. Our team sees emergency preparedness as yet another ounce of prevention that we do not want to be without. If you lost electricity – could you find a flashlight in your house and fresh batteries? Have you stored water, safely, for you and your family: 1 gallon per person, per day, for 3 days, at a minimum. Do you know that once you do shut off the gas to your house that only a gas employee can turn it back on? Do you have an out-of-state contact, to the east (Idaho and eastward), that family can contact so they know you’re okay? Do you have shoes beside your bed and maybe an old bike helmet that could be helpful if you have to navigate broken glass and scattered furnishings in your home?

Spending large sums of money is not necessary to be prepared. Go to respectable websites and learn about how YOU can be prepared. The Red Cross website has several good articles about emergency preparedness.

Take the NET training by registering at the Portland Office of Emergency Management. Right now the spring training has started and I know that the fall training is full. Until you are able to take a NET training, consider taking a first aid class. Put together necessary items in your kit that you wouldn’t want to be without in any type of emergency (don’t forget the chocolate!). Talk to your neighbors; get to know them. They will be a great resource and source of mutual comfort in emergency, big or small. Consider becoming a ham radio operator. And please remember to set aside emergency food and water for your pets.

The Beaumont NET team is a great community resource of educated and trained people (as best we can), who know the value of knowing your neighbors, and being prepared.

Help us, and your neighbors, by being prepared and storing emergency water, food and other supplies for you and your family.

*Team Leader of Beaumont Wilshire Neighborhood Emergency Team, liz@keithcd.com*
