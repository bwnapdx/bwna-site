The neighborhood association has unanimously supported the city’s efforts to address dangerous driving in the so-called Wistaria curve. Transportation officials have recommended speed bumps on a portion of Northeast Wistaria Drive in response to a series of crashes and neighborhood concerns.

The city plan calls for three speed bumps on Wistaria between Cesar Chavez Boulevard (formerly 39th Avenue) and 42nd Avenue. In addition, the city would install raised crosswalks to the north across 41st at Alameda and Klickitat and to the south across 42nd at Stanton. The raised crosswalks are identical to the speed bumps, but painted as crosswalks.

The city would install 22-foot speed bumps that rise three inches, the most gradual it uses. They are designed for vehicles to travel comfortably at 20 to 22 mph, said Mark Lear, the city’s transportation safety program manager. If Wistaria wasn’t part of TriMet bus route 75, the city probably would have recommended 14-foot speed bumps, which are more dramatic and require drivers to slow to about 17 mph to comfortably travel over, Lear said.

The city will notify nearby residents of the plans, conduct a second meeting and look for a general consensus before moving forward, Lear said. That meeting had not been set by the time this newsletter went to press. The speed bumps could be built as soon as this fall or next spring.

At the June 13 general meeting of the neighborhood association, Lear presented the city’s plan. Five residents of Wistaria spoke in favor of the speed bumps. A resolution to support the city’s efforts passed unanimously.

The street has a history of bad accidents, especially as drivers head south into the curve from NE 41st and Alameda. City engineers say the downhill curve has a “negative superelevation,” which pushes vehicles toward the curb. In the worst accidents, vehicles have hit utility poles, fire hydrants and even a house. Residents regularly have their cars sideswiped. Some residents have built walls or placed boulders in the parking strip to prevent more damage.

Residents have periodically asked the city for help with the problem and renewed that effort about 2-1/2 years ago. Earlier this year, the city did a study that measured traffic counts and speeds. The study found that 66 percent of drivers exceeded the 25 mph speed limit and 15 percent drove 30 mph or faster.

The work on Wistaria would be included as part of a project to create bike boulevards on Klickitat Street and Alameda Boulevard. At about $2,500 per speed bump or raised crosswalk, the work along 41st/Wistaria/42nd would cost the city about $15,000.

The street is eligible for speed bumps because 41st/Wistaria/42nd is not a major emergency response route, such as NE 33rd or Fremont Street. Board member Jim Karlock raised concerns about delayed fire response. The bumps would slow emergency vehicles slightly, but the city would be sure that response times continue to meet standards and the Fire Bureau would review the plans, Lear said.

Residents first suggested a permanent speed readerboard to slow traffic. But transportation planners said speed bumps would be far more effective at reducing speeds in the long run and less expensive to maintain.
