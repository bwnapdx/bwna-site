*Monday, December 12, 7:30pm — Bethany Lutheran Church, NE 37th and Skidmore*

Come to the December 12 meeting for treats from BW businesses and to give your input and help in developing a **NE Portland Walking Map!** Rich Cassidy from the City will be presenting this great project on creating a walking map and business coupon book. We need volunteers and neighborhood contacts to help with the guided walks and bike rides the City will be doing as part of the Ten Toe Express and Portland By Cycle Campaigns. The City will also be doing a shop locally (and on foot) business coupon book—free of charge to local businesses.

## Program Summary

Each year the City of Portland Transportation Options Division selects an area to offer outreach and programs on bicycling, walking, carpooling and riding transit. In 2004, they selected the Greater Interstate Corridor which included 6 neighborhoods and about 12,000 households. In 2005, they selected the Southeast Hub and distributed (by bike) free pedometers, walking kits and other materials to over 5,000 households.

The before and after survey results showed a 10% reduction in drive alone trips, a 21% increase in walk trips and a 44% increase in pedometer use. The Options Target Area for 2006 will be what they are calling the Portland Northeast Hub. This area includes the Sullivan’s Gulch, Hollywood, Irvington, Grant Park, Sabin, Alameda, **Beaumont Wilshire**, King, Vernon, Concordia, Woodlawn, East Columbia neighborhoods. The target area includes approximately. 25,000 households and 50,000 residents.

Through a variety of communication strategies and hands-on tours and outreach opportunities they strive to reach all residents at least three times and ideally ten times with information related to all the options for getting around Portland including by bicycle, foot, transit, carpool, and other transportation choices.

The primary goals for the project are:

- Reduce vehicle miles driven by area residents and employees
- Increase trips by walking, biking, transit and carpooling
- Reduce drive alone trips
- Increase awareness to help increase acceptability of all modes of travel
- Increase neighborhood mobility and livability
- Increase safety related to bicycling, walking and taking transit
- Use transportation incentives and programs to support local businesses and economic development
