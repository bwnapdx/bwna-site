**From urban chickens, goats and bees to year-round food gardening, edible landscaping and cheesemaking, Urban Growth Bounty provides the tools for the “urban homesteader.”**

Presented by the City of Portland’s Bureau of Planning and Sustainability, new partners and well-known local experts are on board for the 2011 Urban Growth Bounty sustainable food education series, including Oregon Tilth, Naomi Montacre, Glen Andresen, and Josh Volk. Classes for all skill levels are offered from February through June and held at locations all over the city. Thanks to last year’s popular classes, more than 700 local residents learned how to transform their yards into “urban farms,” preserve their harvest and keep chickens and bees. This year’s roster includes advanced cheesemaking, chicken, goat and beekeeping, and innovative gardening, permaculture and edible landscaping classes. What we eat and how our food is produced, packaged, shipped and disposed accounts for at least 13 percent of human-caused carbon emissions in the United States. Growing food and raising animals that are complementary to food gardening saves money on food bills and offers families the freshest food possible. Backyard food farming also helps reduce carbon emissions that cause global warming. Portland’s goal is to attain an 80 percent reduction in carbon emissions by 2050.

Class schedule and on-line registration is available at www.portlandonline.com/bps/ugb. Questions? Please send an e-mail to food@portlandoregon.gov.
