The snow had been falling all afternoon and into the evening. The grass in the backyard was completely covered and just about everything was covered in white. On this recent winter evening I decided to take the dog for a walk. I bundled up as Cooper sat anxiously at the front door. Finally I put Cooper on his leash and we headed out into the quiet and frosty neighborhood.

As we crossed the street and traveled on, the neighborhood seemed to glow. The streetlights and illuminated Christmas decorations reflected off the snow and lit up the neighborhood as if it were a full moon. The ambient light was bright enough to notice the footprints and tracks of others that had walked before me this same evening. It occurred to me that the footprints in the snow reveal a neighborhood that enjoys walking. To test my theory Cooper and I walked block after block looking for a sidewalk without tracks. We found none. Whether people were out for exercise, the ambience, or to see whose kid built the best snowman, our sidewalks are well used.

At any time of the year, our sidewalks are one of the best assets of our neighborhood. Aside from enjoying a scenic winter walk, in the spring I enjoy strolling to see whose daffodils and tulips are blooming. In the summer I study the chalk art masterpieces drawn by the neighbors kids. And when the cool weather arrives in fall, I look forward to the blazing red maples and the scarlet and bronze oaks set against a dark green curtain of towering Douglas Firs.

Sidewalks are the common thread that keeps the neighborhood fabric intact. It is where we greet our neighbors, share gossip and the latest news, it is where children play and were we go for a stroll or a breath of fresh air. Take my word for it – or better yet, go take a walk.
