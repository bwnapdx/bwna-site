Labor Day weekend is generally accepted as America’s symbolic “finale to summer”--but, technically, of course, summer still has a ways to go after September 6th. So what makes Labor Day weekend feel like it’s the end of summer? Answer: the beginning of the academic year, when thousands of Oregon kids bid their fond farewell to a summer of outdoor fun and family vacations and return to their respective schools buildings. For most, it’s with mixed emotions--sad to lose the freedom and flexibility of summer, but, at the same time, excited to return to the learning process, the social scene, and school-sponsored activities. This would seem to be a universal phenomenon, but different cultures approach the transition from vacation to school in different ways. In Russia, for instance, the first day of school--known as “The Day of Knowledge”--always falls on September 1st and is more of a holiday festival than academic orientation--with kids, parents, faculty, and dignitaries gathering together at schools in celebration of education and country. Not a bad way to “begin” the year. But that, after all, is essentially what September is in our culture as well: a new beginning.

So it is in our neighborhood. Students return to their daily morning walk or ride to Beaumont Middle School, school buses make their rounds, young athletes run through the park and around our streets in training for the upcoming meets...Change and renewal are palpably in the air.

Our neighborhood association is no exception. Coming off of the National Night Out Picnic--which substitutes for Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Associations (BWNA) August General Meeting--September ushers in a new season of Board meetings (held on the second Monday of odd-numbered months) and General meetings (held on the second Monday of even-numbered months--except for August), at Bethany Lutheran Church as before--but with a new starting time for both: 7:00pm instead of 7:30pm. And come October, be on the lookout for lawn signs around the neighborhood (primarily in Board members’ yards) encouraging residents to attend the October 11th General Meeting--our first one since last June! Lots of catching up to do, for sure.

So if I’m asked when the annual BWNA calendar begins, September is the first month that pops into my head. But that’s not the only correct answer. As with cultures that celebrate multiple “new years”--e.g., traditional Judaism--BWNA has different beginnings at different times of the year for different reasons. Thus, besides September’s “beginning,” there’s December’s Winter Holiday/New Year’s General Meeting “beginning,” April’s Taste of Beaumont/BWNA Elections General Meeting “beginning,” June’s BWNA Board Retreat “beginning,” and back to August’s National Night Out Picnic’s “beginning”--which only goes to show that for BWNA, beginning anew is an ongoing process.

I invite you to jump in at any time and lend a helping hand in shaping the future of our neighborhood.
