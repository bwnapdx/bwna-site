The 60-member Wilshire United Methodist Church, located at 3917 NE Shaver, reaches out to the Native Americans in the Portland community.

“Our congregation was looking for a new focus, and we had the most interest in Native American ministry,” says the Rev. Lois Wagner, Wilshire United pastor.

A great number of Oregon’s Native American population lives in the greater Portland area. This compelled the Native American Comprehensive Plan of the Methodist United Church to award Wilshire a $8,300 grant to start an urban native ministry. With the grant Wilshire United has reached out to native groups in the area. They provide meeting space, support a Native American Parent Group with child care, enhance the involvement with the Native American campus ministry in the area, and support the Native American Youth organization.

“Right now, the ministry is integrated with the congregation at Wilshire because that is what the Native Americans at the church wanted,” Wagner says. The ministry has become a place where native people can gather and not be put down for their culture, says Ed Edmo, a ministry member. “We can cook our own native foods, enjoy fellowship, and sing our songs.”

In addition to the weekly gatherings, the Native American ministry has a fifth Sunday service, where members meet for worship, fellowship and food, all provided from a native perspective. “The church allows us to do it our way. It might take a while to get the numbers, but the feeling is good,” says Edmo.
