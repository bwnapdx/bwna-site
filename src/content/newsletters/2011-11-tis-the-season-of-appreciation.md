“You can’t always get what you want…” was the first part of a refrain made famous by the Rolling Stones in the 60s. The thought concludes with: “…but if you try, sometimes you get what you need.” Pretty savvy advice from a very young Mick Jagger, but I would add a cautionary note: “Watch what you wish for!” Case in point: If we had our druthers, what natural setting would we choose to live in, other than the one we’re living in now? For starters, imagine residing in Hawaii or San Diego, or the Bay Area—all significantly warmer and sunnier than Portland much of the year. And how about places like New England or Colorado or Zion National Park?—spectacularly brilliant in their fall colors and regularly picturesque during winter snowfalls. The possibilities are endless. But as a guy who was raised in California, periodically visits our older daughter and her husband in New York City, and has traveled the world a bit…I’m inclined to leave well enough alone and stay put. Consider the Northwest’s upside: four distinct seasons, comparatively mild weather (absence of tornadoes, hurricanes, blizzards, massive flooding, etc.), extensive greenery (resulting from all that rainfall), and as a bonus, summers that are relatively low in humidity and therefore less prone to bug infestations (e.g., no baseball game delays or cancellations due to flying insect invasions!). On balance, I’d say we come out ahead…way ahead.

Appreciating what we have (as opposed to lamenting what we don’t have) is a mainstream theme this time of the year. With Halloween in the rearview mirror, Thanksgiving coming up fast, and winter holidays not far behind, the focus is on family and personal well-being—both of which are inextricably linked to the neighborhood in which we live. And that’s where the neighborhood association should find its calling, right?-- providing forums and services aimed at facilitating neighborhood well-being. But there’s a catch: Residents must opt to participate, and my observation is that BWNA is often underutilized, and perhaps a bit underappreciated as well. The solution? I think the key is communicating more effectively with neighbors about what BWNA has to offer.

Awareness Question #1:

Do you know that BWNA has a Web site (www.bwna.us) and that on that Web site is a blog with information about local issues, activities, and events? You can get information out to the community on the blog or in the Newsletter by e-mailing it to editor@bwna.us. And at the top of that website home page are informational categories to click on, one of which (“Links”) provides phone numbers for a wide range of organizations and services.

You can also sign up for bwna@bwna.us on the home page, which hooks you up to our neighbor-to-neighbor e-mail information loop (including timely announcements & news).

Lastly, you can air your views in the Newsletter by submitting a “Letter to the Board” of 500 words or less on issues of local interest. Send your “letters” to editor@bwna.us.

Awareness Question #2:

Do you know that concerns about vandalism, traffic safety, and proposed projects affecting our neighborhood (municipal or commercial) are the kinds of issues that BWNA is actively engaged in addressing? There’s strength in numbers, and BWNA has established a reputation as an effective organizational vehicle for neighborhood advocacy. BWNA also serves in the planning and implementing of neighborhood events & activities, such as Taste of Beaumont, National Night Out Picnic, and graffiti cleanups.

Conclusion:

Explore the possibilities, get involved, “and you just might find...you get what you need!”
