The June/July Letter from the Editor urged us to keep pollution to a minimum this summer, and one simple way is to eschew the use of gas-powered lawn equipment such as leaf blowers. Two-stroke engines – which leaf blowers, lawn mowers and weed-whackers all have – send as much as 30 percent of the fuel mixture out with the exhaust, unburned. A California EPA report determined that a typical home leaf blower operated for one hour produces 145 times more hydrocarbons, 7.5 times more carbon monoxide and 11 times more particulates than driving a light duty vehicle at 30 mph for an hour. Higher powered blowers used by professional landscapers pollute drastically more.

Then there are the clouds of dust and pollen blown around, futilely moved from one spot to another. Residents with allergies are sent running to close windows and wait inside while the blowers do their unnecessary thing.

In addition to the air pollutants, blowers’ noise contributes stress to people and wildlife, alike. Although their decibels may be within Portland’s legal limits, hired lawn crews typically use a lawn mower, weed whacker and blower, often all at the same time, creating cumulative noise levels of extreme, illegal intensity. Many cities have outlawed blowers or severely restricted their use.

What to do instead? Use people-powered, calorie-burning devices like rakes, brooms and push mowers, or hire landscapers who agree to use the same. Minimize your turf: Those patches of green surrounding most houses may be strongly rooted in the American consciousness, but a little scrutiny reveals their true colors. Fossil fuel-dependent lawns command more labor, money and chemicals than ever before and caring for these monocultures seems more like combat: We apply pesticides that kill millions of birds, insects and amphibians a year and over-apply synthetic fertilizers that can kill soil life and leach into ground water. Then we mow, whack and blow with polluting machines to get picture-perfect, homogenized, impeccably unnatural and damaged ecosystems. If tending a lawn with manual tools sounds exhausting, grow low-maintenance evergreen trees and shrubs – preferably native – instead of lawn.

One last thought [or sidebar?]: The amount of gasoline spilled every summer by Americans filling gas-powered lawn equipment exceeds by 50 percent the amount of oil spilled by the Exxon Valdez. [Ted Steinberg, American Green: *The Obsessive Quest for the Perfect Lawn* (New York, W.W. Norton & Co., 2006)]
