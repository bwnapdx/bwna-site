Seventy years have brought many changes to the Bryce Street block. I have lived on this street since March 1st, 1939, when my parents bought our house with a $200 down payment.

Probably the oldest house in this block is the one first owned by the Rei family. They bought it in 1924 for $1600 and raised six children there. The block between NE 36th and 37th has four houses on either side of the street. In 1939 there were 18 children in those eight houses. None of the mothers worked with the possible exception of Mrs. Carlson, who may have helped her husband at the fountain in their drug store. Not every family owned a car and none owned two cars. The children pretty much occupied the streets and had a wonderful time playing Red Rover, Red Rover, Come Over, Kick the Can, Hide and Seek and other outdoor activities. Today there are fourteen cars parked on the block and too many cars passing by for children to safely play street games. And who would ever expect that helmets, elbow and knee pads would be required equipment in an effort to have fun?

In 1939 there was only one family who owned a dog. Now there are more dogs on the block than children. Our neighborhood has always encouraged community activities, and it’s just a short jog to the corner of Fremont and Alameda and some wonderful shops and restaurants.

In the building which now houses Wilshire Market there was a grocery store at the east end (Schafer's Wilshire Market) and Carlson's Drug Store at the west end. The soda fountain there was a great place for a frosty drink after trudging up the hill from Grant High School. Certainly no high school kid I knew was driving his own car. We all walked. At Schafer's, a family could run up a tab and pay at the end of the month. I think the only business that allows a tab now is a bar, and you most certainly have to pay by the end of night.

Bryce Street was not my parents’ only choice for a house in 1939. We also considered a house at the corner of 39th and Glisan. I feel very lucky that my family decided to purchase a house on 36th and Bryce, and that I have been able to be a part of this community for so many years.

I truly love my neighborhood, and I encourage you to love and take care of your neighborhood too! Get to know your neighbors and care for them. Together, we can create memories that last forever.
