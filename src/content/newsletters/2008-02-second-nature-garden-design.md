Growing up in the Midwest, BW resident Eileen Stark knew she wanted to work with nature and animals. Following work in the biological and animal protection fields, Eileen turned her reverence for flora and fauna into her own business, Second Nature Garden Design where she works with nature toward sustainable design.

The basic principle behind sustainability is for humans to live successfully as part of nature. “We live in a time of tremendous human activity and our actions are overwhelming the planet. One good thing we can do is turn our lawns into gardens that not only look beautiful but balance our taking with giving back. Every choice we make impacts someone or something downstream,” Eileen explained at her NE Wisteria Drive garden that incorporates many trees, ornamentals – native species as well as “favorites I can’t be without” – and edibles.

Although she claims the yard still needs much work, her vegetable garden is producing even during these winter days and fruit trees ready to bud out, are everywhere. She doesn’t use any chemicals. “We never kill anything but exotic weeds and try to compost almost everything on site,” she says. Her clients are encouraged to continually enrich the soil and eschew chemicals as well.

Eileen specializes in edible landscapes, native habitat gardens and water-wise designs. Most of her designs encompass all three, like her own garden, where plants are situated according to their needs. Curving beds delineated by old bricks in the backyard contain mostly native species. “Plants to me are more than just ornamental and I always consider how they provide for the birds and insects and other wildlife. Many people don’t appreciate native plants but they will thrive in urban gardens and their quiet beauty is just wonderful. They’re also very low maintenance.”

Although she creates designs mainly for Portland residences and school grounds, she hopes to get involved with larger eco-restoration projects in the future.

Eileen is also a free lance writer, blogs for Ecometro.com, and volunteers for various animal-related endeavors. She can be reached at 503-467-8545, or visit her website, www.sngdesign.net for more information.
