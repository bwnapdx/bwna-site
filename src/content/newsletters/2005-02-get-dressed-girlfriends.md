Six years ago, as eBay lured enterprising traders to its lucrative, wide-open frontier, Bunny Scalf and Kimberly Makris came to be rivals. In the business of hunting down clothes to sell at auction on the Internet, the two were forced to stake out and defend their thrift-store territories, guard precious finds from each other, and keep separate databases of their customers worldwide.

Problem was, they’re sisters. The two, who often jump to complete each other’s sentences, finally saw the light in October 2003. “We couldn’t compete against each other,” Kimberly says. Bunny continues, “So we said, let’s join forces.” Thus began a busy period of setting up an office for their “home consignment service.” While the two still made their weekly rounds at the thrift stores, they also had girlfriends who engaged them to help clean out their closets, turning the underused contents into cash.

With more than 5,000 customers between them, a brick-and-mortar presence was the logical next step, so the sisters bought **My Girlfriend’s Closet**, the shop formerly called Bonnie’s, bulking up the inventory, establishing regular business hours, and maintaining a constant flow of commerce. “We keep prices reasonable,” Kimberly says, “so things will move quicker.” The duo, who both live in Northeast Portland, take in about 50 to 100 pieces a day, which ensures high turnover among goods ranging from a collectable St. John suit and vintage jewelry to shoes and fragrances.

“We still do eBay,” Kimberly says, as Bunny adds: “But this is so much better because we get to deal with people directly.” Open six days a week for both browsing and consigning, their shop is at **4443 N.E. Fremont St., 503-281-4459**.
