If you are looking for a job—or have ever looked for a job—you know your appearance is critical at that first interview. A local non-profit, Dress for Success, understands that too and, for 11 years, the agency has been helping low income women who do not have money for clothes, find the perfect outfit to make that first impression. Located in the Hollywood District, many northeast Portlanders volunteer as personal shoppers to help the women select clothing and prepare for the interview.

Mamie Powell from North Portland came to Dress for Success after struggling to find work for over a year.  She was on the verge of homelessness and battling depression, low self-esteem and the urge to return to drinking. A social service program referred her to Dress for Success when she requested some interview clothing. “From the time I entered the door, I was treated like a queen,” she says. “At a time when I felt hopeless, my personal shopper made me feel like I could conquer the world. And so I did.”

The suits are just the beginning of a long term relationship with the women. Dress for Success provides interview assistance, mentoring, resume writing, job skills development, job search assistance, personal development, assistance with housing and health care, and a Professional Women’s Networking Group. Most importantly, the organization offers a community and a new sense of belonging for women who have been isolated by physical and sexual abuse, drugs, poverty, divorce, and appearance.

Mamie now works for the State of Oregon helping others find employment. “Dress for Success is a community of women who restore hope and a sense of self worth to other women. They help us reach our highest personal and professional goals and they stay with us as we hit obstacles and achieve success,” notes Mamie.

Dress for Success began by helping 15 women a month in a tiny office; the agency now serves between 250 and 300 women a month in a large store on NE 37th. “We are thrilled to be able to help so many women during this economic downturn,” says Barb Attridge, Executive Director. “Our greatest challenge right now is finding the funding to continue this work”. If you want to help Dress for Success Oregon (www.dressforsuccess.org\oregon), you can volunteer, contribute, or drop off professional clothing at 1532 NE 37th Avenue.
