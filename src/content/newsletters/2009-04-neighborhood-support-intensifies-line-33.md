In an effort to shore up its budget, TriMet is considering reductions in service--including a proposed removal of the Bus #33 “Fremont” route that runs through the heart of the Beaumont-Wilshire business district and school transport areas. Predictably, many neighborhood residents and businesses have protested the elimination of Line #33 by testifying at TriMet “open house” forums and sending e-mails and letters to management. Additionally, Bob Ueland, Chairman of Central Northeast Neighbors--on behalf of CNN’s coalition of eight neighborhood associations, including our own--wrote to TriMet “strongly objecting” to any curtailment of #33 service.

The TriMet response has also been predictable. Jenny Jones from “Customer Satisfaction” replies that consideration of service reductions is undertaken “with great reluctance and only because we see no other viable alternative…It remains our goal to provide quality transit service…in a financially sustainable way during these difficult times.”

Amidst the controversy, the presidents of BWNA and the Beaumont Village Business Association-- prodded by phone calls and e-mails from concerned constituents--garnered unanimous support from their respective Boards of Directors for the drafting of the following letter to TriMet General Manager Fred Hansen:

*Dear Mr. Hansen,*

*On behalf of the Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood, we strongly urge the full retention of Bus Line #33, which is a lifeline for many businesses and residents represented by our two neighborhood associations--the Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association and the Beaumont Village Business Association. As presidents of these associations, we have received numerous e-mails and phone calls imploring us to get the message across to decision-makers at TriMet that the #33 bus line is an essential contributor to the economic and social well-being of the neighborhood it serves.*

*Our residents say it best. Here is a sampling of their comments on the issue (via e-mail to us):*

*“…Canceling the #33 would effectively take our retail corridor off of the transportation grid and could have a devastating impact on the small business community that our neighborhood relies on…This is a popular line that is essential to the vitality of our neighborhood.”*

*“…It would be a travesty if #33 were cut. Many students attending Beaumont Middle School rely on the service…there is no direct line other than the 33 to Emanuel Hospital for residents of Irvington, Alameda, Beaumont-Wilshire, and Rose City Park…(and) many disabled riders depend on that direct access to get to needed services. All alternatives require at least…two transfers.”*

*“#33 is a vital part of our neighborhood of young families and small businesses…”*

*If there were ever a bus line to spare from the budget ax, this is it.*

*Thank you for your time and consideration on this matter.*

*Sincerely,*

*Al Ellis--BWNA President Kate Fischer--Beaumont Village Business Association President*

This is the letter Al Ellis received from Fred Hansen in reply to the above letter…

*"The list of service reductions is proposed, not final. Your comments will help us develop the final service reduction plan. TriMet will hold a public hearing on April 7 from 4:00pm to 7:00pm in the Portland Building auditorium, second floor at 1120 SW 5th Ave. Public comments are accepted through April 10, 2009. I will make final recommendations after public hearings, for consideration and action by the TriMet Board in April and May."*
