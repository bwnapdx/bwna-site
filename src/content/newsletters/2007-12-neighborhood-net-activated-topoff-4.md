The U.S. Dept. of Homeland Security recently conducted the most comprehensive counterterrorism exercise to date: Top Officials 4 (TOPOFF 4). This was one in a series of congressionally-mandated exercises that involved more than 15,000 participants from government, international partners and the private sector in a full-scale, simulated response to radiological dispersal device attacks. Participants included Beaumont Wilshire’s own Neighborhood Emergency Team (NET) volunteers!

The exercise began on October 15 and focused on five key areas to enhance inter-agency coordination, planning and preparedness:

• Prevention: Test the handling and flow of operational and time-critical intelligence between agencies to prevent a terrorist attack.

• Intelligence/investigation: Test the handling and flow of operational and time-critical intelligence between agencies prior to, and in response to, a terrorist incident.

• Incident management: Practice the full range of existing procedures for domestic incident management of a terrorist weapon of mass destruction event and improve top officials’ response capabilities.

• Public information: Practice the strategic coordination of media relations and public information in the context of an incident of national significance.

• Evaluation: Identify lessons learned and promote best practices.

It wrapped up on October 20 when NET volunteers played their role. At 8:30 a.m. the Portland Office of Emergency Management’s (POEM) NET Coordinator Lawrence Behmer contacted 13 NET team leaders, instructing them to activate their trained volunteers. NETs then reported to one of ten staging sites throughout the city where they were assigned to canvass nearby residences and disseminate information about the exercise and how to “shelter-in-place” in the event of an actual emergency. Shelter-in-place means to take immediate shelter where you are—at home, work, school or in between—usually for several hours.

According to Behmer, “Rumors fly and misinformation runs rampant at times like this. NET volunteers will go door-to-door answering questions, dispensing emergency preparedness and basic crime prevention education.” Additionally, emergency volunteers asked a brief series of questions to better gauge residents’ knowledge in emergency response, first aid and related topics.

To learn more about Portland’s NET program and future free trainings, please visit www.pdxprepared.net. For additional tips on disaster preparedness or to learn more about shelter-in-place protocol, visit www.RedCross.org or www.FEMA.gov.
