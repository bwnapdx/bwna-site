Marilyn was looking for a way to give back to the community two years ago when she drove by her old school, Beaumont Middle School.

“It looked absolutely appalling,” she says. The rhododendrons were brown. Thistles grew everywhere. And the lawn was full of 18-inch weeds.

She wasn’t the only one who noticed. Liz Casson-Taylor became the Principal of Beaumont about the same time, and it wasn’t the curriculum or the staff or the school band that people wanted to talk about.

“The first thing people said was, ‘Do something with the outside. It looks terrible,’” Casson-Taylor says.

Marilyn happened to drop by the school and left the Principal a note. She wanted to volunteer as a groundskeeper. It was literally an answer to Casson-Taylor’s prayers.

“It was like she fell from the sky,” Casson-Taylor says. “It’s just been a real blessing to our school.”

Marilyn, who asked that her last name not be published, adopted the school’s gardens. She lives in the North Tabor neighborhood, but none of the schools near her home needed help. She noticed Beaumont on her drives to New Seasons grocery store. It was a nice coincidence that Beaumont was her alma mater, “So long ago that I won’t tell you,” she says.

Marilyn spends two hours twice a week during the summer and once a week during the winter on the gardens. Her best friend and partner Alfred often joins her. He helped turn a “gross weed patch” into a flower bed by spading for a half-hour each visit. Marilyn’s crowning achievement is what the school calls “Beaumont Hill” at the corner of Fremont Street and 42nd Avenue.

Passing drivers roll down their windows and express their gratitude. Local business owners have shown their appreciation. During a fire drill in fall 2009, a class of students thanked her. “I have been thanked more times than I can tell you,” she says.

But Marilyn claims that she does the gardening for selfish reasons. “I get what I call a spiritual high by working in the garden,” she says. “Any worries I have are gone during those two hours. Any unhappiness is gone.”

The only downsides are the occasional vandalism to flowers and recently hurting her leg when she got too “rambunctious” with a shovel.

Marilyn, who still works 32 hours a week, doesn’t have a cell phone and uses the money she would otherwise spend on that to pay for the bright perennials, annuals and evergreen shrubs that she’s bought for the gardens. Those include a Norway spruce, a miniature pine and a black dragon Japanese cedar. She also bought a trimmer to keep the borders of the grass neat and hired a landscaping crew to get the lawn in order. “This is how I love to spend my allowance,” she says.

The Portland Public Schools have only two full-time groundskeepers for 84 schools and a total of 100 properties, says Matt Shelby, a district spokesman. That means mowing and basic pruning are all that get done on a regular basis. And because the district doesn’t use herbicides, the weeds can easily get out of hand.

“I would say our schools depend tremendously on the efforts of volunteers,” Shelby says. He adds that he’s a big fan of Marilyn’s work.

That goes for Casson-Taylor. An anonymous volunteer painted the school’s doors. That has made a huge difference combined with Marilyn’s work.

“We get a lot of compliments about how the school looks,” Casson-Taylor says.

Others have stepped up to make the school look better. The school participates in Project Community Care, when volunteers show up on a Saturday in late summer to spruce up the grounds. And Marilyn is quick to credit others, including a neighbor who helps with mowing and the father of a student who spent a Sunday spreading lime.

Marilyn plans to continue her work through the summer of 2012 because she’s very excited about some new plants next season. After that, she is hoping that someone else takes over. Those who are interested can go to the neighborhood association website at www.bwna.us to find out how to help.

She highly recommends the feeling of accomplishment. “It’s like being a mother,” she says. “I get the joy of creating something beautiful.”
