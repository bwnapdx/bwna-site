With summer moving season drawing to a close and families settling in for a new school year, the question of “neighborhood livability” takes on particular relevance–especially with respect to “desirability factors” that influence where we choose to live.

To be sure, sets of factors vary by person and by family, but commonalities exist as well. Take for example the following five broad categories (not necessarily in rank order): housing, schools, natural environment, business environment, and neighborhood engagement. Using these commonplace criteria as our rubric, how might Beaumont-Wilshire fare in terms of “livability?” Let’s take a look:

Housing: With apartments and condos in relatively short supply, single-family dwellings are the norm. But so is variation–from million-dollar homes on the hillside to modestly-priced cozy bungalows. Shapes, sizes, and affordability run the gamut. Boring, it is not.

Schools: Located in the heart of the Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood, Beaumont Middle School has earned a reputation as one of the premier middle schools on Portland’s East Side–both in terms of academic excellence and racial mix. And given the first-rate staff, dynamic leadership of Principal Elizabeth Casson-Taylor, and dedicated parent involvement, it’s easy to see why.

Natural Environment: Start with beautifully-maintained Wilshire Park–a home to coexisting squirrels, birds, trees, dogs, kids, walkers, runners, ballplayers, et al. Not surprisingly (according to Park Maintenance Supervisor Peggy Glascock), Wilshire is one of the most utilized parks in all of NE Portland. Couple the park’s flora and fauna with verdant yard gardens throughout the neighborhood, and it’s no mystery why so many people gravitate to our area for their daily walks, strolls, runs, and rides.

Business Environment: Fremont Fest and the 42nd Avenue Street Fair attest to the vitality and creative energy of Beaumont-Wilshire’s two business communities. The Beaumont Business Association has grown in leaps and bounds over the past two years, expanding from a handful of Fremont Street merchants to over 50 members, while the NE 42nd Avenue Business Association has been collaborating with three intersecting neighborhoods (Beaumont-Wilshire, Cully, and Concordia) with the goal of establishing 42nd Avenue & Alberta Court as a kind of inter-neighborhood nexus.

Neighborhood Engagement: Perhaps the most compelling factor is the degree to which Beaumont-Wilshire residents feel connected to each other– both as neighbors and as a neighborhood– a product of neighbor-to-neighbor interaction and neighborhood association activism. Taste of Beaumont, National Night Out Picnic, graffiti cleanups, and collective action on local issues are examples of what can be accomplished through individual initiative, intra-neighborhood networking, and community cohesion.

Thus, a neighborhood’s “livability” is largely determined by and reflected in its residents’ shared values. In Beaumont-Wilshire land, that translates into beauty, diversity, and caring.
