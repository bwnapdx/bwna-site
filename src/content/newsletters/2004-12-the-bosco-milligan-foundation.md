Established in 1987 by Jerry Bosco and Ben Milligan, the Bosco-Milligan Foundation is one of Oregon's largest and most active architectural preservation not-for-profits. Since 1992, the Foundation has presented over 160 programs and served the needs of more than 35,000 people interested in the preservation of our built environment.

The mission of *educating people to preserve our building heritage* is carried out in a variety of ways. We present hands-on building restoration workshops, neighborhood walking tours and programs on all aspects of building-related heritage—all geared for people interested in history and preserving the character of their homes and other buildings.

Bosco and Milligan's vast and varied collection of architectural artifacts, of which we are the stewards, is the largest (40,000 square feet) in the western United States and one of the five largest in the nation.

The collection includes many one-of-a-kind pieces from demolished buildings and has numerous examples of decorative plaster, fireplace mantels, tiles, wood windows and just about everything else that was ever designed to be a building element.

The Foundation has a long-term commitment to the *"Cornerstones"* project, which identifies and documents building resources that reflect the African American heritage of Portland. Two editions of *Cornerstones: Buildings of Portland African American History* have been printed to date.

The Architectural Heritage Center, the only facility of its kind in the northwest will open in early 2005.
