During the spring and summer, interaction with neighbors is a common occurrence. Whether it’s visits to Wilshire Park, walking dogs, jogging, strolling the sidewalks, bicycling the streets, dining at a Fremont cafe or tending our yards, opportunities abound for socializing with the people next door or down the street. But with the onset of early darkness and commencement of autumn rains, residents find themselves spending more and more time in the shelter and security of their cozy homes which often translates into diminished communication with neighbors. Of course there are always exceptions. Just look at the brave souls who venture out into the elements for all kinds of reasons.

A humorous example of this took place on our street last month on election night. As my wife tells it, she was driving down our street and about to pull into our driveway just as the national media had declared Obama the winner, when all of sudden she spotted our neighbors—both retired and almost never outside their home after dark—dancing with each other in the middle of the street! After parking the car in the garage, my wife walked over with a big smile and asked the couple what was going on. They answered, “We promised ourselves 4 years ago that if and when our choice for President ever won again, we would celebrate by dancing in the street, and the time is finally here!”

Such delightfully unexpected encounters with neighbors are, of course, few and far between. But when they do occur, both neighbors and neighborhood benefit from the connection. To the extent that neighborly relations are cordial and caring, our day-to-day quality of life is enhanced and security strengthened. The resulting goodwill forms the foundation for a community where reaching out and looking after each other’s best interests become the norm.

With this in mind and the winter holidays fast approaching, I encourage you to bring yourself, your family—and a friendly neighbor, if the spirit moves you—to BWNA’s annual December (8th) Holiday General Meeting at Beaumont Middle School in rm. 204, beginning at 7:30. Help yourselves to sweets and refreshments, meet new neighbors, become informed, and lend your voices to shaping the future of our neighborhood.

Happy Holidays!

*–Al Ellis*  
*President BWNA*
