With the exception of those among us with pollen and grass allergies, the blossoming of the flowers and greening of the lawns give cause for celebration; and even the sneezy, puffy-eyed sufferers can’t help but marvel at the kaleidoscopic beauty of our spring. As the flowers open up and grass grows thick, as temperatures moderate and sunlight expands, Portlanders emerge from their cocoon of winter and into the great outdoors of the Pacific Northwest. Soon hundreds, then thousands--via auto, bicycle, boat, and train--are whizzing away on the weekends to spectacular scenic locations--like the Gorge and the Coast. But living in the Beaumont-Wilshire neighborhood, one might ask, what’s the big rush?! On a daily basis, the sidewalks and streets team with baby carriages, joggers, dog-walkers, garden-browsers, shoppers, and vehicles of all sorts--not to mention our own little forested playground--Wilshire Park--which on any given day is venue to dog running, toddler swinging, picnicking, and Little League--just to name a few. How fortunate we are to have such a wide array of outdoor recreational options in our own backyard!

Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association (BWNA) is proud to serve as steward for this “blossoming” community. Organizing together as neighbors, our Association strives to preserve the positives, combat the negatives, and enhance the overall livability of the neighborhood. However, such laudable objectives are only as effective as our residents’ willingness to become involved in their implementation, and you can do your part by volunteering to play an active role in BWNA. How? Well, there’s no better time to get started than at our “Taste of Beaumont” General Meeting on April 12th! (see page one). This is BWNA’s annual gala neighborhood celebration that features free cuisine samples from your favorite local restaurants and cafes, along with lively entertainment provided by the talented Beaumont Middle School Jazz Ensemble. But there’s more: This is the evening that residents vote to fill vacant BWNA board and officer positions--which is the life blood of our neighborhood association. At least four board positions will be open this year, and I ask you to give serious consideration to serving. It’s a two-year (renewable) term, with two basic requirements: a desire to help our neighborhood and the ability to attend meetings on the second Monday evening of most months.

In the meantime, enjoy the beauty of Mother Nature locally in your own way--albeit, perhaps, with a handy packet of Kleenex close by!
