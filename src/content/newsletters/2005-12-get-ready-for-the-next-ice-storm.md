Ice storms are common in Portland and can cause prolonged power outages, disrupted telephone service, automobile accidents, and the closure of schools, stores, and businesses across the city. Here are some tips from the Red Cross and FEMA so you’re prepared the next time an ice storm strikes.

- Assemble a 72-hour Disaster Supplies Kit (see below).
- Insulate exposed water pipes with a “pipe sleeve” or install UL-listed “heat tape,” “heat cable,” or similar materials. Even a few layers of old newspaper can prevent frozen pipes.
- Drain water from swimming pool and sprinkler supply lines following manufacturer’s directions. Do not put antifreeze in these lines unless directed.
- Remove, drain, and store outdoor water hoses. Close inside valves supplying outdoor hose bibs. Keep the outside valve open so that any water remaining in the pipe can expand without causing the pipe to break.
- Keep non-clumping kitty litter on hand—you can use it to create temporary traction on outdoor walkways. (Rock salt will melt ice on walkways but can also damage vegetation and concrete.)

The Red Cross recommends keeping enough supplies in your home to meet your needs for at least three days. A typical 72-hour kit includes:

- One gallon of water per person per day and food that won’t spoil or require much cooking.
- A change of clothing and footwear and one blanket or sleeping bag per person.
- A first aid kit that includes your family’s prescription medications.
- Emergency tools such as a battery-powered radio, flashlight and extra batteries.
- An extra set of car keys and a credit card, cash or traveler’s checks.
- Sanitation supplies.
- Special items for infant, elderly or disabled family members.
- An extra pair of eyeglasses, if needed.

Store these supplies in sturdy, easy-to-carry containers such as backpacks, duffle bags or covered trash containers.

For more disaster preparedness tips, visit www.RedCross.org or www.FEMA.gov.
