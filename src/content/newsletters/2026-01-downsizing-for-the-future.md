There comes a time in life when you realize you will not, as you believed in your teens, live forever. While that realization came to my husband and me long before now, at 80 and 67 years old, respectively, preparing for our potential infirmity and eventual death moved up on our priority list recently.

Circumstances propelled this into action after my beloved mom passed away last August. She had lived in our ADU for the last four years, and suddenly it was silent and dark, void of my mom's indefatigable joyful spirit and contagious laughter. I couldn't look upon the ADU from our home without experiencing, as a dear friend said, "a tsunami of grief."

Then, soon after, longtime friends downsized and moved to the ADU, where we now reside. Is there some pain in this process? Yes, but it isn't insurmountable. For those of you who might be considering downsizing, here are some things we've learned:

- Once both of our minds were made up to make this move, it was easy to part with once-coveted items.
- It's gratifying to give things to people who will treasure them. For example, the Cape Water Buffalo head, the animal of which was bagged by my husband's father in the 1950s and served time in a Dallas, Ore., bar before hanging out with us for 20 years, is going to my husband's nephew — and he's thrilled. Whew!
- It's been equally gratifying to sell our treasured stuff to people we didn't know, but you could see from the smile on their faces that they were going to give them a good home.

The best outcome from this move, however, is that I no longer feel sadness about the ADU. Instead, I feel it's my turn to bring joy and laughter to those who venture across the threshold of our tiny home.
