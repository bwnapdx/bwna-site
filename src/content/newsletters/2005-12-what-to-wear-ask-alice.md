Alice Dobson, the mastermind behind the **Sofada** label, has designed and made clothes for as long as she can remember. Growing up on the coast in Gearhart and Seaside, she capitalized on her love of fabric so well (“my friends called me boring because I loved to sew,” she remembers), that by age 15 she had made customers of friends and family members.

In Los Angeles, where she enrolled in the Fashion Institute of Design and Merchandising and worked for other designers, she remembers them asking her, “Alice, are you sure you want to do this?” Her mentors wondered whether the manic days of telephone calls, everyday emergencies, and the details of running a business would scare her off, and yet, here she is constantly alternating between her back-room sewing studio and a desk outfitted with phone, calendar, and receipt book in her cozy storefront on Fremont. Sofada just marked its three-year anniversary and incidentally isn’t far from Dobson’s father-in-law’s jewelry business, Alexander’s.

Education received, Dobson, then 24, returned to Portland, where—lucky for local women—she discovered a sour job market. “I had to open the shop because I couldn’t get a job,” she says, noting that not a day goes by that she doesn’t draw on the lessons learned in Los Angeles.

Dobson calls fitting women with beautiful clothes, and therefore a good attitude, her favorite part of the profession: “I design things for women, and I love that it makes them happy.” The shop is lined with colorful ready-to-wear apparel and accessories of her own and others’ design, from corduroy circle skirts and kids’ ponchos to leather handbags and from scallop T-shirts and fitted blazers to baubles created by local jewelry makers. She’s happy to customize her creations to the client, and Sofada garments can be made in all sizes and fabrics.

Sofada hours are 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. Wednesday-Saturday; 4623 N.E. Fremont St., 503-445-2006.
