I was in Washington, DC two weeks ago when my wife called me to say the first daffodil had bloomed. Two days later it was 4 daffodils. All winter we have been looking forward to see the results of planting 100 bulbs last October. Planting bulbs in the fall is one of the best ways to get ready for the arrival of spring.

I don’t know what makes me happier—the smug contentment that I thought of something this good so long ago or the pure sight of those first yellow blooms. I guess it is a combination of both—thinking ahead and then enjoying the moment. The flowers blooming around the neighborhood symbolize that thinking ahead is a trait we all share. Occasionally, however, we could use a few reminders. Here are some things to keep in mind as you get ready for spring activities in the neighborhood:

- When you grab the leash remember to bring a plastic bag to pick up after your dog. Your neighbors will appreciate the consideration as they tip toe through their tulips.
- Coveting the greener grass on the other side of the fence? Be mindful when using fertilizers and weed control on your own lawn. Fertilizers and pesticides can run off in rain events and be harmful to aquatic life in rivers and streams.
- If the warm weather inspires a spring clean up, call Metro Recycling Information at 503-234-3000 or check out www.metro-region.org/recycling. Your may be surprised at what you can recycle and also find a safe place for old cans of paint and chemicals.
- And as for that dusty bicycle, if you have been waiting for the sunny day to go for a spin, please be aware of cars on the road, especially at unmarked intersections. Drivers should also take notice, as there will soon be more bicyclists sharing the road with you.
