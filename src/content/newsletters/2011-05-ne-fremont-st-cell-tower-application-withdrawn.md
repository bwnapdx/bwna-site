About a year and a half ago, neighbors in the vicinity of NE Fremont and Alameda received notification of plans to build a cell tower on Fremont St. adjacent to the Wilshire Market. From the first neighborhood meeting on the issue it was clear that a significant number of residents opposed this plan. Over the course of the next 18 months neighbors held firm in their opposition, covering the neighborhood in yard signs, meeting with City officials and scheduling public events to raise awareness of the issue facing neighborhoods across Portland. Hundreds of neighbors turned out at these meetings and events and contacted the City to let them know they expected them to do more to protect their communities.

RespectPDX was formed by a core group of Beaumont-Wilshire neighbors to advocate for a more respectful and responsible approach to siting cell towers in residential neighborhoods. The group expanded to include concerned Portlanders from all over the City. RespectPDX filed a legal challenge to the Fremont St. application arguing that in approving the location, the City had ignored its own ordinance. That case was about to be heard in County Court in late February, when neighbors were notified that Clearwire, the company who hoped to build the tower, had withdrawn its application for the site along with applications for other sites in residential neighborhoods.

Clearwire is facing a number of significant business and legal issues that may have influenced their decision to withdraw, but the unified voice of Beaumont-Wilshire neighbors undoubtedly helped keep a number of planned cell towers from being built in front of people’s homes. Neighbors have continued to have formal and informal discussions with various City Hall staff as well as participating at the City of Portland’s Strategic Broadband Plan meetings. Applications may have been withdrawn, but Beaumont-Wilshire neighbors plan to continue advocating for an ordinance that regulates the placement of cell towers in a respectful and responsible way.
