I thought I do a good job at talking – my husband and friends would say so. But as the number of listeners increases, so does my need to increase my ability to “speak well”

Recently, a friend invited me to a Toastmasters’ meeting. . After I was greeted by friendly club members, the meeting began with members reciting the mission statement: *Provide a Mutually Supportive & Positive Learning Environment in which every member has the Opportunity to Develop Communication & Leadership Skills, which in turn foster Self-Confidence & Personal Growth.* Toastmasters gives people an opportunity to improve public speaking skills.

As a guest, one feels no pressure to participate. At my first meeting, I volunteered to speak during “Table Topics” – a segment where members talk 1-2 minutes about unknown topics. It appeared easy and I began talking. At what was to be the point of my story, I froze and said, “This is harder than it appears. I will be back.”

Since joining last summer, I was nominated for a board position and have been giving 5-7 minute speeches and serving as a Toastmaster. I enjoy the club members’ adherence to the mission by supporting new and veteran members. Speech topics are endless, entertaining and informative. I have heard a speech by a mother in support of her rap-star son, and a speech on how to make Thai salad rolls.

If you would like a taste of Toastmasters, visit a club and see for yourself. One meets every Saturday from 9-10:15 am, at Rose City Park United Methodist Church, 5830 NE Alameda Street. The meetings follow a strict agenda and end promptly at 10:15. Bring your coffee and start your weekend with some Toast – Masters, that is!
