What began in December as a gathering of about a dozen Beaumont-Wilshire(BW) residents at a last-minute postponed informational meeting by Clearwire concerning an intention to locate transmitting equipment atop a tall metal pole on property owned by Wilshire Market (NE 37th and Fremont St.), just feet away from residents’ homes, morphed into one of the most controversial neighborhood issues in recent history. Following the postponed meeting, the residents organized a committee--which they eventually named “RespectPDX”--with the goals of educating the public and stopping the project. Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association (BWNA)President Al Ellis and Vice President Bill Markwart attended some of the committee’s meetings, which were held at Bethany Lutheran Church, and invited its leaders to address the BWNA General Meeting in December and Board meeting in January, also held at the church. A vote was taken at the December General Meeting to direct BWNA Land Use Committee Board members Michael Rounds and Dave Anderson to further explore the issue and report back with recommendations at the January Board meeting. The result was a decision to hold a special BWNA public meeting later in January devoted exclusively to the “cell tower” issue.

Meanwhile, Clearwire had made the decision to reschedule its informational meeting for January 13th at the Beaumont Middle School library. BWNA and RespectPDX collaborated in publicizing the meeting throughout the neighborhood and in the press, and the result was an unexpectedly large crowd--mostly from the BW neighborhood, but from nearby neighborhoods as well--which overflowed into the hallway leading to the library. Consultant Kevin Martin represented Clearwire, David Soloos of the Office of Cable Communications represented the City of Portland, and Al Ellis was called on to moderate. For over three hours the mostly anti-cell tower crowd assailed Martin and Soloos--sometimes to the point of verbal abuse (to the chagrin of moderator Ellis)--accusing Clearwire and the City of putting the community in jeopardy for the sake of profit and revenues, respectively. The emotional meeting immediately drew the attention of the print and television media, and the once local issue became a city-wide story.

It was then that the BWNA leadership made the decision to hold a Special General Meeting on January 28th to decide what the position of the Association should be on Clearwire’s proposed project. Again, publicity was widespread, and another large crowd packed the Beaumont Middle School library. However, both Clearwire and the City declined invitations to be represented at the meeting, and the result was a relatively calm and systematic discourse leading to the almost unanimous approval of a BWNA resolution opposing  Clearwire’s project.

Since then, BWNA has distributed its resolution to local officials, neighborhood and business associations, and interested media. RespectPDX has put up lawn signs and posters, sponsored the showing of an anti-cell tower-technology movie (called “Full Signal”) at the Hollywood Theater, and even demonstrated at the Wilshire Market site on Superbowl Sunday--all in an effort to keep the tower from becoming a reality in the BW neighborhood. (As of press time for this issue of the Newsletter, the City had neither approved nor denied Clearwire’s request for a permit to begin construction, and the owners of Wilshire Market remained firm in their decision to lease their land to Clearwire for the construction of the tower.)

## Resolution Passed

**At Special General Meeting, January 28, 2010**

Residents of the Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood met in the Beaumont Middle School library and voted by a margin of 46-1 to approve the following resolution regarding Clearwire’s proposal for a wireless facility in the neighborhood:

Whereas, Fremont Street is a Priority 4 residential street for purposes of locating wireless facilities and requires a finding that no other less intrusive site is possible. We are not convinced that Clearwire has performed its due diligence and met that burden of proof; and

Whereas, Clearwire’s equipment cabinets are noisy and the company is slow to address violations of the city noise ordinance in other neighborhoods. This cabinet would be even closer to single-family homes than other locations where this has been a continuing problem; and

Whereas, the health, safety, and peace of mind of Beaumont-Wilshire residents and the livability of the neighborhood are fundamental concerns of the Neighborhood Association; and

Whereas, the Portland City Council has expressed concerns about the health impact of wireless facilities and unanimously passed Resolution 36706 calling on federal agencies for more study into the potential health impacts of RF wireless emissions; and

Whereas, the site selection and approval process unduly restricts neighbors from a formal opportunity to comment on or influence the location;

Now, therefore, be it resolved that the Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association opposes Clearwire’s plans for a wireless tower on Northeast Fremont Street near 37th Avenue or any Priority 4 residential street and recommends that the City of Portland Reject the company’s request for a tower and adjacent equipment cabinet.

Respectfully submitted,
