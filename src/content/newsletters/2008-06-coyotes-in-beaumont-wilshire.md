You may have heard about Portland’s coyote problems, but did you know they have been spotted in Beaumont Wilshire? I wouldn’t have believed it myself until I saw a pair of coyotes in our yard on Alameda Ridge on May 8. The Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife says coyotes look for green “pockets” in the city where there is a source of water. Apparently, no city agency is currently keeping a record of sightings.

Coyotes live in burrows, under downed trees, or in culverts, and are very opportunistic. They prefer small rodents, but will eat birds, unsecured garbage, compost, fruits and vegetables and the occasional free-roaming pet. Coyotes mate for life, and a brochure published by Audubon Society says they have a “compensatory, density-dependant breeding rate” - the more humans intercede to remove or kill urban coyotes, the more they will reproduce and fill in the vacated territories.

In Oregon it is illegal to relocate a coyote. Traps are dangerous to pets and other wildlife, so the best defense against these predators is to discourage their presence. Keep garbage cans and compost bins securely covered and pick up fallen fruit. Remove brush that will afford cover for coyotes and places for rats to breed. If you see a coyote in your yard, make a lot of noise to scare it off. Never feed them.

Although generally not dangerous to humans, coyotes may consider your pet a tasty meal. Our visitors left behind a lot of what appeared to be cat fur, and although our own cat is a different color, she hasn’t been home since. To keep your cat or small dog from meeting a similar fate, keep it indoors from dusk until dawn, when coyotes are most active, and don’t leave pet food outdoors.
