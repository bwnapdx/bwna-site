At the end of January, attendees at a Special Meeting of Beaumont-Wilshire Neighborhood Association (BWNA), held at Beaumont Middle School, approved a resolution in opposition to a proposed project by Clearwire to install a cell tower and transmitting equipment adjacent to a residential area in the vicinity of N.E. 37th and Fremont.

But it wasn’t until the end of May, following months of contention between the company and BW neighborhood residents, that Clearwire finally submitted its official application to City Hall’s Office of Cable and Franchise Management, requesting permission to proceed with the project. Once the 70-page-+ application was made public, BWNA’s Ad Hoc Cell Tower Committee analyzed the document and made an assessment in regards to the January BWNA resolution. With endorsements from the Central Northeast Neighbors (representing a coalition of eight neighborhood associations) and the N.E. 42nd Ave. Business Association, the following objections to the project were explained in a cover letter accompanying the resolution and sent by BWNA President Al Ellis to David Soloos at the Office of Cable and Franchise Management, Mayor Sam Adams, and City Commissioners:

*Clearwire has not provided convincing evidence that it has performed its due diligence and met the burden of proof in determining that there is no viable Priority 3 residential site for this project.*

*While shifting this project to another part of our neighborhood is not a satisfactory solution, a Priority 3 site is preferable to one in a Priority 4 residential area--which is, according to City Commissioner Amanda Fritz, “a last resort” for installation of this kind of tower and equipment. While suggesting in its application the possibility of a Priority 3 alternative in the vicinity of N.E. 33rd Ave. and Fremont St., Clearwire has not, as required, documented a systematic and exhaustive search for all viable Priority 3 sites for this project and should be held accountable for doing so prior to a final decision on the application.*

*There is no assurance that the noise level of Clearwire’s cooling equipment, though vaulted, will not pose a noise problem for the single-family homes surrounding the proposed site.*

*As a condition of approval, Clearwire’s cooling equipment should be subject to the most current City noise control ordinances, which would require compliance not only with current noise ordinance standards, but with future standards as well. Testing of the vault installation should extend throughout the day and night, weekdays and weekends (since nearby residents would be impacted 24/7), and, prior to approval of the application, provide convincing evidence to City Noise Control Officer Paul Van Orden that the equipment is not in violation of the noise code.*
