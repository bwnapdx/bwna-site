There has been increased attention on the issue of radon in homes on the Alameda Ridge. Many people probably saw the report on KPTV news on April 5th addressing the issue particularly for Alameda Ridge, referred to as “Radon Ridge” in the broadcast. For some this appears to be a new issue to consider. Actually radon is present in some level all throughout the greater Portland Metro area.

What is radon? It is a naturally occurring radioactive gas that is the decay product of radium, an element found commonly in granite bedrock. As this gas decays, it releases particles that can harm lung tissue. Radon is considered the leading cause of lung cancer in non smokers. As this gas seeps up from the soil it enters homes through cracks and holes in slabs, crawlspaces and basement foundations. Since it is odorless and tasteless there is no way to detect it without testing. Radon is measured in pCi/L or picocuries per liter of air. For test results above 4 pCi/L it is strongly recommended that remediation be done.

Testing is easy to do. Since the gases collect in the lowest part of the home, simple passive test kits should be placed in the 1st floor or basement. The kits can be purchased at hardware stores or at the National Radon Program Services on the EPA Website. The short term test kits cost between $10 and $15 dollars. The readings are then sent to a lab for analysis. If the readings are above the recommended levels, remediation should be handled by a certified mitigation specialist. Mitigation typically costs less than $2000. It usually involves placing piping and an inline fan underneath the basement floor or crawl space and rerouting the radon laden air around the living area and releasing it into the atmosphere above the home.

Just as every homeowner or prospective home buyer already knows about sewer scopes and oil tank decommissioning, radon testing is a common sense due diligence activity.
