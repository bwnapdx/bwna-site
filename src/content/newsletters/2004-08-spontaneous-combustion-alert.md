On a June Friday morning at 2:15 AM, my neighbor behind me and I were both awakened by two alert neighbors who were returning from a swing shift, and saw flames in our back yards. When we raced out, we saw ten foot high flames on our shared wood fence that were beginning to ignite a 60’ evergreen tree.

My neighbor’s wife called 911 while we both battled the flames with hoses. Our efforts kept the fire from spreading laterally, but with 10-20 mph winds, the tree was not something we could cope with as the flames climbed. Sparks were beginning to fly to other parts of the yard and towards our rooftops. Fortunately, the fire crew arrived in about 4 minutes and doused the entire area. The one tree was destroyed. We were extremely fortunate because there are about a dozen similar sized trees on our block that are clustered in an urban ‘forest’, and had more ignited, as many as five houses would have been at risk. Only because of the wind direction were other trees and our houses preserved.

The cause: spontaneous combustion in my neighbor’s yard waste pile. Unbeknownst to me, they had been piling grass clippings, prunings, and leaves under the tree in a 10x10 ‘crib’ for some time and these materials ‘self-combusted’.

Here is what I learned and want to share:

1. Remove all such debris from under trees.
2. Do not place debris / compost near flammable fences or structures.
3. Create a managed compost environment, which is turned and worked rather than just piled haphazardly.

Many thanks to the “C” crew of Portland Fire Bureau Station 14, who were fast and efficient and saved our ‘urban forest’ and our yards from more damage.
