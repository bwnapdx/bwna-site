As the effects of this economic crisis are felt more and more by working families, students, and seniors, two-thirds of corporations doing business in Oregon are still paying just $10 a year in income taxes based on a 1930s law. This January, voters will likely have a chance to change that. Large corporations are currently gathering signatures to repeal two crucial bills passed by the legislature that raise the $10 corporate minimum income tax (for the first time since it was lowered from $25 in 1931) and increase the marginal tax rate on personal income above $250,000 for households. The tax increases protect small businesses and middle-class families while raising approximately $733 million in order to limit cuts to education, public safety, and healthcare. If the corporations gather enough signatures, Oregonians will vote on the measures in January 2010. Here’s what the tax-fairness package does: Increases the $10 corporate minimum income tax. The new minimum is set at $150 for corporations with less than $500,000 in Oregon sales, and is capped at $100,000 for corporations with more than $100 million in Oregon sales. The minimum is tiered and equals approximately one-tenth of one percent of a corporation’s in-state sales. Small business making less than $500,000 in Oregon sales, S-corps, and LLCs will pay just $150.

Raises the marginal tax rate on corporate profits over $250,000 by 1.3% (until 2011, when the rate increase drops to 1% and only applies to profits above $10 million).

Raises the marginal tax rate on income over $125,000 for single filers and $250,000 for households by 1.8% (until 2012, when the rate increase drops to 0.9%). For example, a couple making $260,000 will pay an additional $180 per year.

Cuts taxes on unemployment payments to help those who’ve been hurt most by the recession get back on their feet.

If these measures are overturned, lawmakers will be forced to make deep cuts to education, healthcare, and public safety. Voting YES in January is critical in protecting schools against further teacher layoffs and growing class sizes; it is critical in protecting against further cuts to in-home care for seniors, health care cuts, cuts in public safety and other crucial services we all rely on as members of a community. Further, voting YES will help make Oregon’s tax system fairer to working families.
