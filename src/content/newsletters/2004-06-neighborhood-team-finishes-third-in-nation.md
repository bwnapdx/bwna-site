When the BWNA Board agreed to fund Grant High School’s constitutional law team to the tune of two hundred dollars from our modest general fund, we had no way of knowing that we would soon be the proud sponsors of a neighborhood team that would place first in the state and third in the nation in competition that drew more than 1500 students from around the country.

Our Northeast Portland team, composed of 36 GHS seniors and coached by teacher Diane Thelen-Sager, placed just behind national champion East Brunswick High of New Jersey and second-place winner Maggie L. Walker Governor’s School for Government and International Studies in Richmond, Va.

The contest, called We the People: The Citizen and the Constitution, is directed by the nonprofit Center for Civic Education and funded by the U.S. Department of Education. Teams compete in six groups, each specializing in areas such as roots of the Constitution, history and current constitutional issues.

Each group is grilled by a three-member panel composed of judges, lawyers, congressional staff and constitutional scholars. The teams are judged on six criteria: understanding, constitutional application, reasoning, supporting evidence, responsiveness and participation.

After the dedicated Grant students learned that they had placed among the final 10 teams in the national competition, they left a dance to go back to their hotel and study. The team was awarded an engraved walnut plaque, and each student received a gold medallion.

Grant’s team went to the national competition two years ago but did not make it into final round. Loathsome cross-town rival, Lincoln High School of Portland won the national championship in 1990, 1991 and 1996. Lincoln has won the state competition every year since the contest began in 1987, except for the two glorious years it lost to Grant.

Grant team members took a semester government class from Thelen-Sager and studied the Constitution for hours each week, including meetings with volunteer coaches—many of them lawyers and judges. Six coaches accompanied the team to Washington, D.C.

The victorious students say their studies gave them a deeper understanding of power, politics, democracy and the possibilities for themselves.
