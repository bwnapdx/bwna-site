In 1924, when Fred Elsasser was four, his parents bought a 45 x 143 ft lot on NE 42nd near Shaver. Fred's uncle Henry bought the adjacent lot. The Elsassers had just arrived to Portland from Saskatchewan, Canada. Fred thinks the lots cost about $200 each. The lumber to build the homes came from a barn that was being disassembled on SE Peacock Lane. The lumber from the barn was old growth timber, and the two Elsasser homes are still on 42nd today.

A year later, the Elsassers moved into their new home. To the east were plant nurseries and Shaver Street did not extend east past 42nd. In 1926, Fred was one of the students of Beaumont School's first class. He spoke little English, since German was spoken at home. He remembers not knowing how to ask his teacher for permission to go to the restroom, so he just quickly ran home and back to class. He graduated from Grant in 1938 and enrolled at the University of Oregon majoring in journalism. For health reasons, he did not serve in WW II, but his three brothers did. His brother William was one of the first to enter Hiroshima after the bombing.

The Elsasser family attended the Second German Congregational Church located at NE 8th and Skidmore. The congregation moved to its current location on NE 55th at Alberta in the 1950's where Fred worships today.

Neighborhood businesses Fred remembers are a service station at 47th and Fremont, a grocery store where Justin's Market is where Fred worked sacking potatoes, and a  drug store where the Alameda Café is now located. As a teenager, Fred also worked at the Beaumont Pharmacy which was located in the Nanwood Honeyman Building, built in 1931 and now called the Dutch Building. In those days, doctors called in prescriptions and pharmacists made the medication by hand. Fred was paid ten cents to deliver prescriptions on his bike. When not delivering, Fred worked the soda fountain. He especially liked to make milkshakes for his girlfriend, Virginia Swearingen.

Fred lives in one of the seven apartments he built in 1965 on the lots behind the two original Elsasser houses. He is blessed with good health and does his own cleaning and cooking. He drives a 1973 Cadillac, "the last model to be made of all metal and it is not for sale!" He is active in the Masons and Shriners. When I asked him why he has lived in this neighborhood for 82 years, he said, "People on the west side are snobby and this has been a perpetually good neighborhood."
