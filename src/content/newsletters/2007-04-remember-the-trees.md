Trees are as essential to life as we know it, so they deserve the utmost in care and preservation. Now, as leaves begin to emerge, is an ideal time to think about the care your household will offer your recently planted (and established) trees this year.

While walking in the neighborhood, we may observe trees that appear to be holding up a big red flag. These trees are victims of a constricting rope or wire that attaches the tree to stakes that have overstayed their welcome. Deadly girdling restricts water and food movement and results when a tree grows into ties. Stakes and ties should be removed one year after planting, although very large trees may need support for up to two years. If in doubt, grasp the trunk and shake it – if the root ball doesn’t move, it is safe to remove the stakes. Even while staked, trees should never be held rigidly in place. If ties become tight, loosen them.

Proper watering will ensure healthy trees. While it’s commendable to conserve water, young trees, especially street trees in full sun, need ample water. Friends of Trees recommend ten to fifteen gallons of water, applied once a week over a period of about two hours, during the hot summer months. Never water shallowly and frequently, as this can lead to other irreversible problems.

Try to maintain about an 18-inch radius of weed-free and grass-free space around tree trucks. On the outer 15 inches of that radius, keeping mulch away from trunk, place a couple of inches of mulch to reduce evaporation and keep weeds down. Don’t use fine chips or sawdust that can tie up nitrogen and decrease oxygen in the soil

The environmental and aesthetic benefits from trees are innumerable, and if you follow these simple but crucial care instructions, your priceless trees will be well on their way to a healthy and, hopefully, long life.
