On the first Tuesday of each August, BWNA throws a party in the middle of Wilshire Park and invites the whole neighborhood. By far the most ambitious undertaking of the year for the association, this National Night Out celebration to promote crime prevention awareness has given rise to one of the largest community potluck picnics in the city.

If you came to this year's picnic on August 5th, you were one of an estimated 300 neighbors and friends who came together to share in the food and fun.

The neighborhood association provided hamburgers, hotdogs, and soft drinks, Rose's Ice Cream (on 42nd) supplied the ice cream, and neighbors brought chips, dips, entrees, and desserts from home.

Also on hand was a fire engine provided by Fire Station 14 for kids to explore and K9 handler Officer Lonn Sweeney showed off his prized dog, Vulcan. Local massage therapist Johannes Venghaus offered free seated massages.

Thanks to the following local businesses for door prizes and other donations to the picnic: Vestiges, Alameda Cafe, Alameda Fitness Center, Ottoman Klasik, Alameda Brewhouse, Americana Frame, Camilla Pure Beauty, Green Dog Pet Supply, Bureau of Environmental Services, Parisis, A Repeat Performance, Albina Bank, and Johannes Venghaus.

A special thanks to Bethany Lutheran Church for providing storage space for picnic supplies and Helen Stewart of Wilshire United Methodist Church for her painstaking hours helping us carry and transport dozens of tables and chairs from the church to the park and back again.

Also in attendance were special guests City commissioner Nick Fish, newly appointed Fire Chief John Klum, Port of Portland Senior Aviation Planner Sean Loughran; CNN Crime Prevention Program Coordinator Michael Boyer; Bureau of Police N.E. Precinct's Commander Jim Ferraris, Lieutenant Tom McGranahan, and local neighborhood officer R. Reagan as well as S.E. Precinct's Commander Derrick Foxworth and Lieutenant Bob Heimbach. All expressed their delight with the number of people in attendance, the array of homemade foods, and the neighborly spirit at the event.

Despite the multitude of people, not all the hamburger meat and buns were consumed, and leftovers were donated to regional food banks. Also, a set of keys was left behind and retrieved by BWNA President Al Ellis. If those lost keys are yours, give Al Ellis a call. (503-287-0477)
