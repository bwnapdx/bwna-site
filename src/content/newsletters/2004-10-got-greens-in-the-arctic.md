Sharing dinner with our next-door neighbors, Tim and Jewel Lehman, is something we’ve come to look forward to in the two years we’ve lived in Beaumont Wilshire. Jewel is an extraordinary cook and I try to hold my own when we reciprocate. But whatever the food, the company is always delightful. On one such evening, a conversation about nutrition and the importance of fresh fruits and vegetables elicited a story from Tim about his solo kayak adventure in the Arctic.

Tim and Jewel both enjoy kayaking but in 2002, in search of a challenge and a unique experience, Tim spent 17 days alone paddling in the frigid waters around Baffin Island taking photos and enjoying the landscape. He packed freeze-dried food for convenience and space restrictions but no fresh produce. One day his stomach was not quite right, so he decide to search out some mountain sorrel (oxyria digyna), munched it and felt better shortly. He said it just felt so good to eat something fresh. I wondered, other than sorrel, what the Inuit did for roughage before the “fly it in” days. Tim said one thing they did was eat the seaweed from the stomachs of the walrus they caught for meat. He continued to describe other edible plants but the sorrel story stuck with me.

As we enjoyed Jewel’s delicious vegetable ragout from a Basque recipe featuring ingredients from the Hollywood Farmer’s Market where they volunteer every Saturday throughout the season, we thought how fortunate we are to live in a neighborhood where there is never a shortage of good greens, even sorrel, if one is so inclined.

Tim and Jewel have lived in Beaumont-Wilshire since 1984 and have a physical therapy practice--Moreland Physical Therapy. For more interesting facts about the Arctic, Tim suggests *A Naturalist’s Guide to the Arctic* by E.C. Pielou.
